# Approvals: which actions need approval, and from whom

Once permissions.md has scoped what the agent can reach, every remaining action is allowed, gated or blocked. This file decides which, who answers, and what counts as an answer; how a run pauses and resumes for that answer is agent-harness humans.md.

## Grade every action first

- **Set each action to always allow, needs approval, or block, and separate reads from writes on the same system.** Reading a calendar can be always-allowed while sending an invitation needs approval; Anthropic uses this pattern in Claude.ai and Desktop because it keeps approvals rare enough that people still read them. (`anthropic-trustworthy-agents`)
- **Grade the customer's own confirmations too, from silent reads to an explicit yes before anything irreversible.** If the prompt doesn't define when to act, ask or confirm, OpenAI says the assistant may call tools too early or ask unnecessary questions. Its voice guide runs read-only lookups without asking when intent is clear, reads exact identifiers back before a lookup, drafts customer-visible messages before sending, confirms amount, target and consequence before purchases, cancellations and payments, offers escalation before anything irreversible, and says an action is done only after the tool call succeeds. (`openai-voice-prompting-guide`)
- **Grade each action by harm and by how easily it's undone, attach a safeguard to each grade, and refuse outright what's too risky to delegate.** Without the reversibility axis, irreversible mistakes land with no chance to intervene: unmitigated Operator made 13 nuisance errors in 100 realistic tasks, 8 easily reversible and 5 irreversible or possibly severe, such as emailing the wrong recipient and setting a wrong medication-reminder date (2025-01-23, OpenAI's own system card), so payments and sending email confirm while stock trades and banking transactions are refused (proactive-refusal recall 94% on a synthetic set). (`openai-operator-system-card`)
- **Inside its scope, the agent takes the least disruptive path and backs up, dry-runs and documents how to reverse before an irreversible step.** The Model Spec's compliant email cleanup shows the list before acting and archives instead of deleting, and treats side effects as real even when it may be in a test. (`openai-model-spec-2026-08-18`)
- **Rate each connector action by whether an attacker could see its effect.** A write whose result an outsider can read is an exfiltration channel (data.md). OpenAI's Lockdown Mode guidance calls any action on untrusted apps, and writes on trusted apps "with broad or uncertain visibility", high risk, and enables a write only if "the side effect is hidden from a malicious actor". (`openai-help-lockdown-mode`)

## Limits live in the tool

- **Refund and credit caps are hard constraints in the tool** (agent-harness tools.md: limits in the handler, and the model never fills an amount). Fin's example: refunds up to $50 proceed without approval and refunds above $50 require human review, going only to the original payment method and never above the order total, because "A refund cap should not be a suggestion. It should be a hard constraint." Fin calls action guardrails the most commonly under-configured category once agents refund or cancel, since a wrong transaction has already gone through. (`fin-ai-guardrails-customer-service`)
- **Check every limit on the state the write would produce, and serialize writes per session.** An agent retries, rephrases and issues parallel calls in ways a person clicking a button never does, so two "add two more" calls stack past a per-request cap; Anthropic's commerce agents check caps on the resulting line and serialize cart writes per session. (`anthropic-commerce-agents-anatomy`)
- **Write a few hard invariants for every flow that moves money, verifies identity or discloses data, and check them on the trace in code** (agent-harness rule 2). No account data before verification passes; side effects only after identity, eligibility, selection, validation and explicit confirmation in the same session; retry caps that escalate; masking (last 4 SSN, last 5 card) that no request undoes. Parloa treats any violation as critical and says "The invariants should not rely on an LLM judge to interpret intent." (`parloa-red-teaming`)
- **Put a hard verification step in the action path for flagged accounts.** After stolen support data was used to impersonate it to customers, Coinbase announced "Flagged accounts now require additional ID checks on large withdrawals" plus mandatory scam-awareness prompts (2025-05-15). The ID check is the control; telling customers that support never asks for passwords, 2FA codes or fund moves is only messaging. (`coinbase-extortion-disclosure`)
- **Log every record edit immutably, and block deletes or rewrites of financial and audit records without an approval outside the conversation.** When the person asking is the one doing harm, input filters see nothing hostile: in Anthropic's 2026 tests agents helped a user hide a payment from investors by editing the asset file, stopping only at fabricating board approval. (`anthropic-agentic-misalignment-summer-2026`)
- **Make every preview free of side effects.** OWASP names "consent laundering through read-only previews", a preview pane that fires webhooks when opened; block network and state-changing calls during preview, and show the expected side effects and their source before the person confirms. (`owasp-agentic-top10-2026`)

## Who may approve, and what counts

- **Approval comes from a record in your system with an approver identity, never from text in the conversation or ticket.** Intigriti's attack walkthrough (2026-09-03), a demonstration and not an observed incident: an attacker's email with ">" quoted lines renders in the operator console as an earlier message from support@acme.com, and with no security measures in place the agent reads it as internal approval and issues a $4,200 refund. (`intigriti-hacking-ai-support-agents`)
- **Never put an automatic approver in charge of consequential tools in a conversation strangers type into.** Anthropic's auto permission policy treats whatever arrives as the user turn as the operator's intent, so relayed customer text can get a call allowed that would otherwise be denied, while tool results and MCP responses are assessed but not obeyed. Set "always ask" on every tool the customer must not trigger unreviewed. (`anthropic-docs-managed-agents-permission-policies`)
- **Where the approval itself is the point, make it impossible to automate and require a person who is present.** Granting access, accepting terms or moving money for a customer prompts on every call: no allow rule, mode, remembered choice or one-tap remote button clears it, and an automated approver's allow becomes a deny (Claude Code's `requiresUserInteraction` tool flag). In logged-in email or banking sessions, OpenAI's watch mode pauses Operator when the supervising user goes inactive or navigates away, and in ChatGPT agent it stays on for the rest of the run once triggered. (`anthropic-docs-claude-code-mcp`, `openai-operator-system-card`)
- **Trigger confirmation from deterministic rules, not the model's judgment, and let the person complete the irreversible step.** Chrome's agent confirms before a fixed list of sensitive sites, any password-manager sign-in and the final payment step, so an injection that fools the model can't skip the confirmation. (`google-chrome-agentic-security`)
- **An approval names the action and what makes it risky, and covers that one action only** (agent-harness humans.md ties it to the turn that asked). Claude Code's permission modes require the message to "name the action and the specific thing that makes it dangerous", so "you can force-push" clears nothing. Most misses in Anthropic's auto-mode classifier came from "approval-shaped evidence", a vague "clean up the PR" or one earlier similar approval read as consent: 17% false negatives on 52 real overeager actions, 5.7% on 1,000 synthetic exfiltration attempts, 0.4% false positives on 10,000 real calls (2026-03-25, Anthropic). (`anthropic-docs-claude-code-permission-modes`, `anthropic-claude-code-auto-mode`)
- **Audit every exception in the approval policy.** An attacker routes exfiltration through exactly the action you exempted: PromptArmor (May 2026) pulled SharePoint files out of Microsoft Copilot Cowork through a poisoned skill, because a Teams message to the active user needed no approval and carried image tags with pre-authenticated download links (vendor-reported "high success rate", including against Claude Opus 4.7). (`promptarmor-copilot-cowork-exfil`)
- **Run deterministic checks on a sensitive call before the approval request reaches a person, and again before it executes.** Otherwise wrong-account or over-cap calls fill your team's queue and train them to decide on autopilot; the OpenAI Agents SDK runs tool input guardrails after approval by default and offers a pre-approval option. (`openai-agents-sdk-guardrails`)

## A confirmation in the prompt is a layer, not the gate

- **Never adopt a reference demo's confirmations as your permission model** (agent-harness rule 2). OpenAI's customer-service agents demo (2025-12-18) confirms before `cancel_flight` only because the prompt says "For cancellations, confirm details and use cancel_flight": no tool carries `needs_approval`, the booking agent works from the confirmation and flight number already in context and is told to "Execute autonomously", and the refunds agent is told to "Operate autonomously" before `issue_compensation`. An injected or confused model calls the tool directly. (`openai-cs-agents-demo`)
- **Give the agent a written confirmation policy, and still gate critical actions in code.** The policy cuts unauthorized actions without removing them. In OpenAI's adversarial workplace evals, exposing the policy cut GPT-5.6 Sol's misaligned outcomes from 18.8% to 8.0% and its unauthorized transactions from 38.0% to 7.4%; GPT-5.6 Luna went from 19.7% to 7.2%, GPT-5.6 Terra from 13.5% to 5.7%, GPT-6 Astra from 3.4% to 3.0% (2026-09-03, OpenAI's own system card). (`openai-gpt-6-astra-system-card`)
- **Measure how often the agent asks before each kind of risky action.** A confirmation the model decides to ask for misses some of the actions that need one. ChatGPT agent asked before 91.0% of relevant actions and 100.0% of permission edits, 99.9% of high-stakes communications and 100.0% of financial transactions (2025-07-17, OpenAI's own system card). OpenAI says confirmations "reduced the risk by approximately 90%" against Operator's baseline mistake rate, and in a separate eval Operator asked with 92% recall on 607 tasks across 20 categories (2025-01-23). (`openai-chatgpt-agent-system-card`, `openai-operator-system-card`)
- **Scope permissions per session in a policy layer outside the model; never rely on per-call dialogs, which users switch to "Always Allow".** Invariant (May 2025): a malicious public issue made Claude 4 Opus pull private-repo data into a public PR through the GitHub MCP server; GitHub couldn't patch it server-side because it's architectural, and a one-repository-per-session rule blocks it. (`invariant-github-mcp-exploit`)

## Allow rules and the gate itself

- **Write narrow allow rules, exact command or tool plus argument shape, and remove broad ones before running unattended.** Anything a broad rule covers never reaches your guardrail: OpenAI found a sizable minority of internal Codex users allowing every command starting with `python` and one config always allowing `codex exec --yolo`, and Anthropic drops blanket-shell, interpreter and package-manager-script rules on entering auto mode. A tool-level "refunds: always" removes the gate the same way. (`openai-auto-review-research`, `openai-codex-auto-review`, `anthropic-claude-code-auto-mode`)
- **Never allow a command runner by prefix, and never constrain URLs or arguments with patterns.** A runner such as npx, docker exec or devbox run executes its arguments as a command, so `devbox run *` approves `devbox run rm -rf .`, and argument patterns miss reordered flags, redirects and variables. (`anthropic-docs-claude-code-permissions`)
- **Test every deny rule against path and alias variants of the command.** Rules that match command text miss those variants. In the Claude Agent SDK, `Bash(rm *)` matches the command as written so `/bin/rm` passes, a single-slash path anchors at the working directory, a `Write(path)` rule never matches, and `allowed_tools=['*']` approves nothing; deny a tool by bare name to remove it from the model's context. (`anthropic-docs-agent-sdk-permissions`)
- **Set the permission mode explicitly on every agent and subagent.** An unset mode can change between SDK releases, and subagents inherit the parent's: in the Claude Agent SDK an unset mode can now start in auto mode (from TypeScript SDK v0.3.286), and subagents inherit a parent's bypass mode. (`anthropic-docs-agent-sdk-permissions`)
- **For an agent nobody watches, list its tools and turn every approval request into a deny.** Pair an explicit tool list with a deny-on-prompt mode (the Agent SDK's `dontAsk`) instead of counting on an approval callback nobody wired up, and remove unneeded tools by name, because calls that need no approval, such as read-only shell and reads in working directories, still run. (`anthropic-docs-agent-sdk-permissions`)
- **Check which tools the platform's permission system covers, and authorize every call in your handler** (agent-harness rule 2). Managed Agents permission policies don't apply to custom tools your app runs (a support agent's refund and account tools), the built-in toolset including bash defaults to always-allow, and only MCP toolsets default to ask. Caller restrictions are guidance: Anthropic says not to rely on `allowed_callers` "as a security boundary", and code calling a tool in a loop isn't re-sampled between calls, so rate-limit and check each call server-side. (`anthropic-docs-managed-agents-permission-policies`, `anthropic-docs-programmatic-tool-calling`)
- **Test the approval gate itself as attack surface.** Two days after Snowflake's Cortex Code CLI shipped, PromptArmor's crafted command syntax passed the validator that decides what needs approval, ran outside the sandbox without approval, and used the victim's live credentials to exfiltrate data or drop tables; fixed in 1.0.25 (2026-02-28). (`promptarmor-snowflake-cortex`)

## Model reviewers at the boundary

- **Keep approve-or-deny in a separate model call with a narrow job.** The main agent "is optimized to complete the user's task", which "creates pressure to treat an approval boundary as just another obstacle to overcome". A denial returns a rationale so the agent finds a safer path (Codex continues successfully after a denial in more than half of cases, 2026-04-30, OpenAI), and the run stops automatically after repeated denials so the reviewer can't be worn down. (`openai-auto-review-research`)
- **A reviewer model can take most approvals off people.** Codex Auto-review (GPT-5.4 Thinking at low effort; 2026-04-30, OpenAI's own report) stops for a person "roughly 200x less often", auto-approves 99.1% of escalated actions, and denied 99.3% of synthetic injection cases, 90.3% of overeager risky actions and 96.1% of MonitoringBench-hard malicious trajectories; counting destructive deletions and data tampering, injection recall drops to 90.2%. In one internal snapshot, 720 escalations produced 7 rejections. (`openai-auto-review-research`)
- **A reviewer is not a guarantee, and it sees only actions that cross the boundary.** OpenAI's red-teaming found cases where Auto-review "could be misled into approving commands", and OpenAI does "not expect this class of system to become a source of deterministic guarantees"; harmful actions inside the boundary, including sends to an allowlisted destination, are never reviewed, and a reviewer from the agent's own family (GPT-5.4 for Codex) shares its blind spots (guardrails.md). Never widen permissions in exchange for a reviewer, and keep money, identity and consent tools with a present person (above). (`openai-auto-review-research`)
- **Switch blocking review on for the whole risky stretch with a cheap classifier, not step by step.** Codex's guardian prompt emits one token: high when the current action has unknown or low authorization or high or critical risk, when an unknown-authorization or critical action happened in the last 5 actions, when high or critical risk is expected in the next 2, or when unsure; high turns on the blocking reviewer and low lets the next calls run unreviewed (repo snapshot, 2026-09-26). Per-step review misses a risky course spread across harmless-looking calls. (`openai-codex-guardian-approvals`)
