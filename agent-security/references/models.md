# Which model resists attacks best

There is no crown to give. Every published number depends on who ran it, which attacks, how many tries and which configuration. No model in any benchmark sits at zero. Read the numbers with the rules below, shortlist from the tables, then decide with your own adaptive red team on your own agent (agent-evals red-team.md). Numbers that vendors publish about themselves and their competitors are in models-cross-vendor.md and models-lab-evals.md. What safeguards add on top of a model is in guardrail-layers.md.

## How to read any robustness number

- **Never compare numbers across vendors' reports or across benchmarks.** Each vendor picks the attack set, the configuration and the competitors. Anthropic runs Claude with its blocking classifiers and fallbacks but no injection safeguards. It runs other models on public endpoints "which may or may not include additional safeguards", and drops models that were targets in Gray Swan's competitions. An aggregator's chart of Google's launch posts labels its set "Transfer Only, No Computer Use" while importing other vendors' overall figures, so its configuration is unclear (2026-09). (`anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`, `benchmarklist-grayswan-ipi`)
- **A static attack set overstates robustness against an adaptive attacker.** The Opus 4.8 fallback had 0 breaks in 1,310 rollouts on Gray Swan's static IPI set. Under the adaptive Shade attacker in coding, it reached 85.73% attack success (2026-09-22). (`anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`)
- **The attack budget changes everything, so read the number of attempts before the percentage.** On Gray Swan IPI, Opus 5's attack success rose from 0.4% at k=1 to 4.8% at k=15 (2026-09-22). In CAISI's injection tasks the average hid a high-impact task, "Downloading and executing a malicious script", so rate risk per task (2025-01-17). (`anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`, `nist-caisi-agent-hijacking`)
- **Cap retries and never offer strangers a free, unlimited test surface.** A low per-attempt rate still compromises many tasks across repeated searches. A black-box TAP attacker built on GPT-5 hijacked GPT-5 on AgentDojo at 4.5% to 4.7% ASR per evaluation. Yet across 4 independent optimization runs, each injection evaluated 6 times, Universal TAP compromised 30.0% of task pairs and single-task TAP "∼14%". Injections optimized on small open models didn't transfer to it (2026-06-09). (`automated-pi-agentic-envs`)
- **Ask which attacker sits behind the number.** Anthropic's k on Gray Swan IPI counts draws from a fixed pool of known transferable attacks. Shade's 200 attempts adapt to the model. On the same 40 scenarios and 200 attempts, Gray Swan's original Shade broke Opus 5 on 2.68% of coding attempts (2026-09-01) and the stronger Shade on 88.92% (2026-09-22). (`anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`, `anthropic-system-card-fable-5-1`)
- **A zero expires when attackers refresh, and one model's number moves between cards.** Opus 4.7 with safeguards was 0% across 148 browser environments (2026-04-16) and 12.4% of scenarios on 129 environments re-sourced against it (2026-06-17). Opus 5 was 2.0% at k=15 on the Q1 2026 IPI set (2026-08-19) and 4.8% on Q1+Q2 with fallbacks (2026-09-01). Compare only inside one table from one card. (`anthropic-system-card-opus-4-7`, `anthropic-system-card-opus-4-8`, `anthropic-system-card-opus-5`, `anthropic-system-card-fable-5-1`)
- **Measure the system you ship, not the bare model.** A safety classifier that reroutes to an older model can become the injection path. Of Opus 5.5's Shade coding requests, 64% went to Opus 4.8, which was compromised 85.73% of the time. Of the 2,872 requests Opus 5.5 answered itself, 0 were compromised (2026-09-22). A harness mismatch made Anthropic re-run every Cowork browser result (2026-08-19). Check whether your provider or router can swap in another model. (`anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`, `anthropic-system-card-opus-5`)
- **Count completed attacks, not diversions, and never bank on clumsiness.** WASP calls attacks that divert an agent but fail to complete the attacker's goal "security by incompetence", visible only in end-to-end evaluations (2025-05-16). It shrinks as agents get better at multi-step tasks. (`wasp-web-agent-injection`)
- **Choose by injection results on your surface, never by capability rank, tier or brand.** CAISI found at least one successful attack on every one of 13 frontier models and no uniform link between capability and robustness (2026-03-23). In Anthropic's browser eval on one harness, raw Sonnet 5.5 was injected on 0% of attempts against 0.09% for Opus 5.5 and 2.55% for Fable 5.1 (2026-09-28). A smaller model can be the more robust one. (`nist-caisi-red-teaming-competition`, `anthropic-system-card-sonnet-5-5`)
- **Robustness can regress between versions and between tiers of one version.** On Gray Swan IPI, Sonnet 5.5 reached 3.4% at k=15 against 1.0% for Opus 5.5 (2026-09-28). GPT-5.4 Thinking regressed on function-call injections from 0.996 (GPT-5.2 Thinking) to 0.978 (2026-03-05). Newer generations have mostly improved, though. In the chart of Google's posts, Opus 4.8 is 8.0% and Opus 5.5 1.0%, GPT-5.6 Sol 27.0% and GPT-6 Sol 10.1% (2026-09, budget not stated). (`anthropic-system-card-sonnet-5-5`, `anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`, `openai-gpt-5-4-thinking-system-card`, `benchmarklist-grayswan-ipi`)
- **Never read a compliance or refusal score as injection resistance.** OpenAI's Model Spec Evals (596 everyday, non-adversarial, non-agentic prompts) put GPT-4o at 72% and GPT-5 Thinking at 89%. OpenAI says the absolute scores "should not be taken too seriously" (2026-10-05). (`openai-model-spec-evals`)

## No model is injection-proof (IPI Arena, 2026)

- **Pick defenses assuming an injection will sometimes get through, so that it hits a permission check, an approval or an egress block.** This is the only independent head-to-head of frontier models in the corpus. Gray Swan with UK AISI, US CAISI, OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta ran 464 participants and 272,000 attempts over 41 scenarios. All 13 models were hijacked. The most robust, Claude Opus 4.5, broke on 13 of 37 behaviors (2026-03-16). (`grayswan-ipi-arena`)
- **Breaks keep coming at a steady rate as attempts grow.** "Break counts increase approximately linearly for all models", so more attempts mean proportionally more successes. A tool judge checked in code that the harmful action happened, and in 32 of 41 scenarios a second judge also scored the agent's reply to the user. (`grayswan-ipi-arena`)

- **Read the table as one benchmark, one threat model.** Thinking was off except for Kimi K2, Gemini 2.5 Pro and Haiku 4.5. Most models drew 130 to 160 unique attackers. Attacks were single-turn with the attacker seeing the conversation, so the paper calls the rates an upper bound on single-turn vulnerability, not real-world frequencies. (`grayswan-ipi-arena`)
- **The weakest surface depends on the model, so test the setting you ship.** Across all 13 models tool use was hijacked most (4.82%), then computer use (3.13%), then coding (2.51%). But Opus 4.5 was weaker in computer use (1.1%) than tool use (0.6%), and Gemini 2.5 Pro's computer use (16.2%) nearly doubled its tool use (8.9%). In Anthropic's own IPI runs, GUI computer use is the weakest surface for its newest models: Opus 5.5 at 2.8% against 0.5% for coding and 0.4% for tool use at k=15 (2026-09-22). (`grayswan-ipi-arena`, `anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`)

## Other independent benchmarks

- **b3 (Lakera with UK AISI, ICLR 2026) scores backbones in isolation on a 0 to 1 vulnerability scale, lower is better.** The score is not an attack success rate. The benchmark replays 210 attacks curated from 194,331 crowdsourced ones 5 times each at one LLM step, so it can't say whether a poisoned document is retrieved at all. Lakera sells AI-security products, and the paper is peer-reviewed. The paper ranked 34 models, some with reasoning both off and on. The rows below are the top of its overall ranking (Figure 2) plus three reference points, and (R) means reasoning on. (`lakera-b3`)

- **Rank candidates on the attack types your agent faces, not on one overall score.** On b3 the best and worst models held across weak, strong and self-judge defenses (claude-haiku-4-5 most secure at every level). They changed across task types such as indirect tool invocation and context extraction, the slice a ticket-reading refund agent cares about. The benchmark's two most capable models, gpt-5.1 and kimi-k2-thinking, ranked 8th and 14th. (`lakera-b3`)
- **Test combined attacks, not one vector at a time.** On Agent Security Bench (13 backbones, 2025-05-30), the mixed attack (direct injection, injected tool output and memory poisoning together) averaged 84.30% success with a 3.22% refusal rate. Direct injection alone reached 72.68% and indirect alone 27.55%. The benchmark's 11 defenses had limited effect. (`asb-agent-security-bench`)
- **Never cite AgentDojo's results page as a model leaderboard.** Its maintainers say it is "not a leaderboard" because models weren't run against the same attacks and defenses. Its newest row is claude-3-7-sonnet-20250219, run 2025-02-24. Use it for defense comparisons on one model. (`spylab-agentdojo-leaderboard`)
- **A public challenge with zero breaks shows a low per-attempt rate, not immunity.** More than 2,000 people sent 6,000+ emails to an Opus 4.6 email assistant, and none leaked the secret or got an unauthorized reply (2026-06-25). The challenge was one-shot, used one strong model and paid at most $1,000. Its owner still doesn't give his agents the ability to send email. (`irarrazaval-hackmyclaw`)

## Agents that publish no numbers

- **Treat an agent vendor with no published injection measurements as unmeasured, even when its docs describe injection controls, and put your own controls around it.** Cognition's security page for Devin covers SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 and its security program, and doesn't mention prompt injection. Its product docs do describe controls: an injection and exfiltration guardrail on user messages, network and MCP allowlists, an OS sandbox and a warning about public-repo triggers. It still publishes no attack success rate, guardrail detection rate or red-team result for Devin or Windsurf (checked 2026-10-06), and its one model eval measures neutrality and refusals (below). An independent researcher showed full compromise of Devin through indirect injection and got no fix timeline after 120+ days (2025-08-06). Nothing published tells you how it behaves under attack, so egress limits, no secrets in reach and approvals have to carry the whole load. (`cognition-security`, `cognition-docs-ai-guardrails`, `cognition-docs-security-profiles`, `cognition-docs-cli-sandbox`, `rehberger-devin-500`)

## Where the answer depends on the case

- **Reasoning or extended thinking helped in most measurements.** With thinking on, Sonnet 5 went from 40.30% to 19.47% of Shade coding attempts and Opus 5 from 3.25% to 0.29% in computer use (2026-09-22). The b3 benchmark found reasoning lowered vulnerability for most models, while size alone didn't (2026-02-24). (`anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`, `lakera-b3`)
- **It hurt or did nothing in others.** In Shade coding, Opus 5 was compromised on 88.92% of attempts with thinking against 85.49% without (2026-09-22). The tiniest b3 models, gemini-2.5-flash-lite and gpt-5-nano, got worse with reasoning. Rehberger saw Opus "thinking" variants as more susceptible in his own tests (2026-04-17, anecdotal). User-pasted instructions are a separate case (injection.md). (`anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`, `lakera-b3`, `rehberger-breaking-opus-memory`)
- **Until your own measurement settles it, run with thinking on where you control it, log the setting you actually run, and measure both modes.** The two bullets above point both ways, and the newest measurement (2026-09-22) leans toward thinking on. Gray Swan's "without thinking" runs on Claude 5 models before the Fable 5.1 card silently used adaptive thinking, because the API enables it when the parameter is unset (2026-09-01). (`anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`, `anthropic-system-card-fable-5-1`)
- **Before building on an open-weight base model, test it inside your own harness for complying with harmful requests and for writing worse code for some users.** Cognition found Kimi K2.7 and K2.6 implemented a demographic-targeted surveillance feature "in all samples" and added identity, camera location and timestamps unprompted, while SWE-1.7, Claude Opus and GPT-5.5 "refused the identical request every time". Cognition cites CrowdStrike (2025-11) and Booz Allen (2026-05) finding Qwen3-Coder "introducing roughly 130% more vulnerabilities" when it believed it worked for a U.S. government agency. Inside its own agent harness it saw no "statistically meaningful difference" across six personas for current models, and suggests the harness's large system prompt may suppress what a raw endpoint shows. Cognition graded its own model, with a GPT 5.5 judge and five runs per model (2026-07-08). (`cognition-open-model-trustworthiness`)
