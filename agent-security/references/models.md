# Which model resists attacks best

There is no crown to give. Every published number depends on who ran it, which attacks, how many tries and which configuration, and no model in any benchmark sits at zero. Read the numbers with the rules below, shortlist from the tables, then decide with your own adaptive red team on your own agent (agent-evals red-team.md). Numbers that vendors publish about themselves and their competitors are in models-cross-vendor.md and models-lab-evals.md; what safeguards add on top of a model is in guardrail-layers.md.

## How to read any robustness number

- **Never compare numbers across vendors' reports or across benchmarks.** Each vendor picks the attack set, the configuration and the competitors. Anthropic runs Claude with its blocking classifiers and fallbacks but no injection safeguards while other models run on public endpoints "which may or may not include additional safeguards", and drops models that were targets in Gray Swan's competitions; an aggregator's chart of Google's launch posts labels its set "Transfer Only, No Computer Use" while importing other vendors' overall figures, so its configuration is unclear (2026-09). (`anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`, `benchmarklist-grayswan-ipi`)
- **A static attack set overstates robustness against an adaptive attacker.** The Opus 4.8 fallback had 0 breaks in 1,310 rollouts on Gray Swan's static IPI set and 85.73% attack success under the adaptive Shade attacker in coding (2026-09-22). US CAISI's attack tuned to the model took Claude 3.5 Sonnet on AgentDojo from 11% (best baseline attack) to 81% (2025-01-17). (`anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`, `nist-caisi-agent-hijacking`)
- **The attack budget changes everything, so read the number of attempts before the percentage.** Retrying five CAISI injection tasks 25 times each raised average success from 57% to 80%, and the average hid a high-impact task, "Downloading and executing a malicious script", so rate risk per task (2025-01-17). (`nist-caisi-agent-hijacking`)
- **A low per-attempt rate still compromises many tasks across repeated searches, so cap retries and never offer strangers a free, unlimited test surface.** A black-box TAP attacker built on GPT-5 hijacked GPT-5 on AgentDojo at 4.5% to 4.7% ASR per evaluation, yet across 4 independent optimization runs, each injection evaluated 6 times, Universal TAP compromised 30.0% of task pairs and single-task TAP "∼14%"; injections optimized on small open models didn't transfer to it (2026-06-09). (`automated-pi-agentic-envs`)
- **Ask which attacker sits behind the number.** Anthropic's k on Gray Swan IPI and ART counts draws from a fixed pool of known transferable attacks, while Shade's 200 attempts adapt to the model. Gray Swan's original Shade broke Opus 5 on 2.68% of coding attempts and a stronger Shade on 90.93%, same 40 scenarios and 200 attempts (2026-09-01). (`anthropic-system-card-opus-4-7`, `anthropic-system-card-fable-5-1`)
- **A zero expires when attackers refresh, and one model's number moves between cards.** Opus 4.7 with safeguards was 0% across 148 browser environments (2026-04-16) and 12.4% of scenarios on 129 environments re-sourced against it (2026-06-17). Opus 5 was 2.0% at k=15 on the Q1 2026 IPI set (2026-08-19) and 4.8% on Q1+Q2 with fallbacks (2026-09-01); compare only inside one table from one card. (`anthropic-system-card-opus-4-7`, `anthropic-system-card-opus-4-8`, `anthropic-system-card-opus-5`, `anthropic-system-card-fable-5-1`)
- **Measure the system you ship, not the bare model.** A safety classifier that reroutes to an older model can become the injection path: 64% of Opus 5.5's Shade coding requests went to Opus 4.8, which was compromised 85.73% of the time, while 0 of 2,872 requests Opus 5.5 answered itself were (2026-09-22); in the Fable 5.1 card every successful coding attack on Fable 5.1 came from its fallback and none of the 2,826 requests it answered itself fell (2026-09-01). A harness mismatch made Anthropic re-run every Cowork browser result (2026-08-19). Check whether your provider or router can swap in another model. (`anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`, `anthropic-system-card-fable-5-1`, `anthropic-system-card-opus-5`)
- **Count completed attacks, not diversions, and never bank on clumsiness.** On WASP, web agents were hijacked in up to 86% of cases but completed the attacker's goal in at most 16%, "security by incompetence" that shrinks as agents get better at multi-step tasks (2025-05-16). (`wasp-web-agent-injection`)
- **Choose by injection results on your surface, never by capability rank, tier or brand.** CAISI found at least one successful attack on every one of 13 frontier models and no uniform link between capability and robustness (2026-03-23). In Anthropic's re-run of its browser evals on one harness, raw Sonnet 5 was injected on 0.47% of attempts against 7.80% for Mythos 5 and 11.15% for Opus 4.8 (2026-08-19), so a smaller model can be the more robust one. (`nist-caisi-red-teaming-competition`, `anthropic-system-card-opus-5`)
- **Robustness can regress between versions and between tiers of one version.** GPT-5.1 Instant blocked 0.575 of email-connector injections against 0.811 for GPT-5.1 Thinking (2025-12-11), and GPT-5.4 Thinking regressed on function-call injections from 0.996 (GPT-5.2 Thinking) to 0.978 (2026-03-05). Newer generations have mostly improved: in the chart of Google's posts Opus 4.8 is 8.0% and Opus 5.5 1.0%, GPT-5.6 Sol 27.0% and GPT-6 Sol 10.1% (2026-09, budget not stated). (`openai-gpt-5-2-system-card`, `openai-gpt-5-4-thinking-system-card`, `benchmarklist-grayswan-ipi`)
- **Never read a compliance or refusal score as injection resistance.** OpenAI's Model Spec Evals (596 everyday, non-adversarial, non-agentic prompts) put GPT-4o at 72% and GPT-5 Thinking at 89%, and OpenAI says the absolute scores "should not be taken too seriously" (2026-10-05). Operator, tuned "especially cautious", beat GPT-4o on StrongREJECT goodness@0.1 (0.66 against 0.37) but answered only 55% of benign over-refusal items against 90% (2025-01-23). (`openai-model-spec-evals`, `openai-operator-system-card`)

## No model is injection-proof (IPI Arena, 2026)

- **Pick defenses assuming an injection will sometimes get through, so that it hits a permission check, an approval or an egress block.** This is the only independent head-to-head of frontier models in the corpus: Gray Swan with UK AISI, US CAISI, OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta ran 464 participants and 272,000 attempts over 41 scenarios, and all 13 models were hijacked; the most robust, Claude Opus 4.5, broke on 13 of 37 behaviors (2026-03-16). (`grayswan-ipi-arena`)
- **Breaks keep coming at a steady rate as attempts grow.** "Break counts increase approximately linearly for all models", so more attempts mean proportionally more successes. A tool judge checked in code that the harmful action happened, and in 32 of 41 scenarios a second judge also scored the agent's reply to the user. (`grayswan-ipi-arena`)

- **Read the table as one benchmark, one threat model.** Thinking was off except for Kimi K2, Gemini 2.5 Pro and Haiku 4.5; most models drew 130 to 160 unique attackers; attacks were single-turn with the attacker seeing the conversation, so the paper calls the rates an upper bound on single-turn vulnerability, not real-world frequencies. (`grayswan-ipi-arena`)
- **The weakest surface depends on the model, so test the setting you ship.** Across all 13 models tool use was hijacked most (4.82%), then computer use (3.13%), then coding (2.51%), but Opus 4.5 was weaker in computer use (1.1%) than tool use (0.6%) and Gemini 2.5 Pro's computer use (16.2%) nearly doubled its tool use (8.9%). In Anthropic's own IPI runs GUI computer use is the weakest surface for its newest models: Opus 5.5 at 2.8% against 0.5% for coding and 0.4% for tool use at k=15 (2026-09-22). (`grayswan-ipi-arena`, `anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`)

## Other independent benchmarks

- **b3 (Lakera with UK AISI, ICLR 2026) scores backbones in isolation on a 0 to 1 vulnerability scale, lower is better, not an attack success rate.** It replays 210 attacks curated from 194,331 crowdsourced ones 5 times each at one LLM step, so it can't say whether a poisoned document is retrieved at all; Lakera sells AI-security products, and the paper is peer-reviewed. It ranked 34 models, some with reasoning both off and on; the rows below are the top of its overall ranking (Figure 2) plus three reference points, and (R) means reasoning on. (`lakera-b3`)

|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Rank candidates on the attack types your agent faces, not on one overall score.** On b3 the best and worst models held across weak, strong and self-judge defenses (claude-haiku-4-5 most secure at every level) but changed across task types such as indirect tool invocation and context extraction, which is the slice a ticket-reading refund agent cares about; its two most capable models, gpt-5.1 and kimi-k2-thinking, ranked 8th and 14th. (`lakera-b3`)
- **Assume a determined attacker gets through within tens of tries, and alert on repeated near-identical attempts.** On ART (Gray Swan with UK AISI, 4,700 curated attacks, 44 behaviors, 19 models), one query broke 20% to 60% of behaviors per model and at 10 queries the rate "approaches nearly 100% for most models", though per-session competition ASR ran from 1.5% to 6.7% by model, "with Claude models exhibiting the highest robustness" (2025-07-28). (`grayswan-art-benchmark`)
- **Test combined attacks, not one vector at a time.** On Agent Security Bench (13 backbones, 2025-05-30), the mixed attack (direct injection, injected tool output and memory poisoning together) averaged 84.30% success against 72.68% for direct injection alone and 27.55% for indirect alone, with a 3.22% refusal rate, and its 11 defenses had limited effect; per-model averages across all attacks ran from GPT-4o-mini 67.55% down to Mixtral-8x7B 19.01%, with Claude 3.5 Sonnet at 56.44%. (`asb-agent-security-bench`)
- **Never cite AgentDojo's results page as a model leaderboard.** Its maintainers say it is "not a leaderboard" because models weren't run against the same attacks and defenses, and its newest row is claude-3-7-sonnet-20250219 at 7.31% targeted ASR (2025-02-24), with gpt-4o-2024-05-13 at 47.69% (2024-06-05); use it for defense comparisons on one model. (`spylab-agentdojo-leaderboard`)
- **A public challenge with zero breaks shows a low per-attempt rate, not immunity.** More than 2,000 people sent 6,000+ emails to an Opus 4.6 email assistant and none leaked the secret or got an unauthorized reply (2026-06-25); it was one-shot, used one strong model, paid at most $1,000, and the owner still doesn't give his agents the ability to send email. (`irarrazaval-hackmyclaw`)

## Agents that publish no numbers

- **Treat an agent vendor with no published injection measurements as unmeasured, and put your own controls around it.** Cognition's security page for Devin covers SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 and its security program and does not mention prompt injection at all (checked 2026-10-06); an independent researcher showed full compromise of Devin through indirect injection and got no fix timeline after 120+ days (2025-08-06). Nothing published tells you how it behaves under attack, so egress limits, no secrets in reach and approvals have to carry the whole load. (`cognition-security`, `rehberger-devin-500`)

## Where the answer depends on the case

- **Reasoning or extended thinking helped in most measurements.** Sonnet 5 went from 40.30% to 19.47% of Shade coding attempts and Opus 5 from 3.25% to 0.29% in computer use with thinking on (2026-09-22); Opus 4.7 coding fell from 92.5% to 60.0% of scenarios, and without thinking Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.6 lost every scenario (100%) (2026-04-16); b3 found reasoning lowered vulnerability for most models while size alone didn't (2026-02-24); Opus 4.5 computer use went from 28.6% of scenarios at standard thinking to 0% extended, while Sonnet 4.5 was at 85.7% either way (2025-11-24). (`anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`, `anthropic-system-card-opus-4-7`, `lakera-b3`, `anthropic-system-card-opus-4-5`)
- **It hurt or did nothing in others.** On ART, Opus 4.6 was injected more with extended thinking (21.7% at k=100) than without (14.8%) (2026-02-05); b3's tiniest models, gemini-2.5-flash-lite and gpt-5-nano, got worse with reasoning; ART found gains marginal for Claude 3.7 Sonnet and negligible for o3-mini (2025-07-28); Rehberger saw Opus "thinking" variants as more susceptible in his own tests (2026-04-17, anecdotal). User-pasted instructions are a separate case (injection.md). (`anthropic-system-card-opus-4-6`, `lakera-b3`, `grayswan-art-benchmark`, `rehberger-breaking-opus-memory`)
- **Until your own measurement settles it, run with thinking on where you control it, log the setting you actually run, and measure both modes.** The two bullets above point both ways and the newest measurement (2026-09-22) leans toward thinking on; Gray Swan's "without thinking" runs on Claude 5 models before the Fable 5.1 card silently used adaptive thinking because the API enables it when the parameter is unset (2026-09-01). (`anthropic-system-card-opus-5-5`, `anthropic-system-card-fable-5-1`)
