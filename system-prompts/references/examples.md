# Few-shot examples

Examples show the model a request and the right response. They fix behavior that instructions leave inconsistent (format, length, tone, a judgment call), and on current reasoning models they can also cost accuracy, so add them on evidence. Tone and persona rules are voice-and-output.md; voice agents beyond their example turns are voice-agents.md.

## Whether to add examples at all

- **Start zero-shot on a current reasoning model, and add an example only when an eval shows a behavior gap it can fix.** OpenAI's reasoning guide says to try the prompt without examples first, and its GPT-5.6 guidance keeps examples only when they encode a product requirement or fix a measured gap; leaner GPT-5.6 system prompts, with repeated instructions and examples removed, scored +10-15% on a sample of OpenAI's internal coding-agent evals, by OpenAI's rough figure, which it calls directional (docs, read 2026-10-22). Its older accuracy guide still explains why examples help non-reasoning models: few-shot took a GPT-4 Icelandic correction task from BLEU 62 to 70 (2024-era). (`openai-reasoning-best-practices`, `openai-using-gpt-5-6`, `openai-gpt-5-6-prompt-guidance`, `openai-optimizing-llm-accuracy`)
- **Diagnose the failure before you pick the fix: missing knowledge goes into context, inconsistent behavior gets examples.** Examples added to a knowledge gap, or documents added to a behavior gap, spend tokens and fix nothing, and OpenAI warns that too much irrelevant context "drowns out the real information and causes hallucinations". (`openai-optimizing-llm-accuracy`)
- **Expect few-shot to hurt some reasoning models (o1-preview, R1) and to help Gemini.** Microsoft found random and kNN 5-shot both scored below a minimal prompt on MedQA for o1-preview (3 runs, temp 1.0, Nov 2024), and DeepSeek recommends zero-shot for R1. Google recommends always including examples for Gemini (2026-10-13), says clear examples can replace instructions, and warns that too many overfit. (`microsoft-medprompt-to-o1`, `deepseek-r1-paper`, `google-gemini-prompt-design-strategies`)
- **Weigh examples by runtime.** Google's Dialogflow CX says example quality and quantity decide playbook accuracy more than instruction precision (at least 1 example per playbook, 4 or more recommended, 2026-10-21), while Anthropic's Claude 5 guidance says the newest models need fewer. On playbook platforms and small models, invest in examples; on frontier models, start from instructions and add examples where a measured failure needs them. (`google-dialogflow-cx-playbook-best-practices`, `anthropic-new-rules-context-engineering-claude-5`)
- **Write a non-obvious conditional rule as an instruction, and show pattern-like behavior with examples.** Stanford's MIPRO (2024, Llama3-8B) found optimizing demonstrations beat optimizing instructions on most tasks, by up to +13% accuracy, but instructions mattered most for a conditional rule (HotPotQA Conditional), and optimizing both was best in 5 of 7 programs. It measured a non-reasoning model. (`stanford-mipro-optimizing-instructions-demonstrations`)

## Where examples go

- **Keep a standing example set in one compact, tagged block apart from the rules, and send examples tied to one task in the user message.** OpenAI's prompt-engineering guide puts few-shot examples in the developer message, in an Examples section after Identity and Instructions, while its prompting overview keeps task-specific examples in user messages as one short block. Neither page says when each applies; how they fit with the role split is structure.md, Which role each piece goes in. (`openai-prompt-engineering-guide`, `openai-prompting-overview`)
- **Put the examples for one request type in the procedure or skill that loads when that case matches.** OpenAI's advice is "Put templates and examples inside the skill", where they cost nothing until it triggers; the runbook pattern behind it is procedures.md, Move procedures out of the system prompt. (`openai-skills-shell-compaction-tips`, `anthropic-cb-sre-responder`)
- **Attach example messages, near-misses included, to a procedure's trigger.** Intercom's examples teach Fin to tell a password reset from a question about login policy (procedures.md, "Steps, triggers and branches"). (`intercom-building-fin-procedures`)

## Writing the set

- **Use 3 to 5 examples that mirror the real use case and span its cases, each in `<example>` tags inside `<examples>`.** Examples too alike teach incidental patterns and untagged ones blur with instructions. Anthropic curates "a set of diverse, canonical examples" instead of a laundry list of edge cases, because, for an LLM, examples are the "pictures" worth a thousand words; you can ask the model to critique the set's diversity or draft more. On a reasoning model, keep worked reasoning out of them (Worked reasoning in examples, below). Whether to go above five, up to many-shot, is weighed under "How many examples" in Where the answer depends on the case, at the end of this file; read it before answering a question about the count. (`anthropic-prompting-best-practices`, `anthropic-effective-context-engineering`)
- **Show only behavior you want, because the model copies details you didn't mean.** Anthropic says Claude 4.x and similar models "pay very close attention to details in examples". Wang et al. measured near-verbatim copying of intermediate steps when an example resembled the live request (2025-09), so keep examples visibly different from each other and from typical inputs, and label them as illustrations. (`anthropic-prompt-engineering-best-practices-2026-blog`, `wang-harm-to-help-reasoning-demos`)
- **Draw examples from production: common cases and edge cases in their real proportions, at the real task's complexity.** Too few patterns narrow the output to them (Eugene Yan, 2024). Amazon's Nova 2 guide asks for directly relevant examples that match the task, and if fixed examples still fail, selects them per request by similarity to the input. (`amazon-nova2-prompt-engineering-guide`, `eugeneyan-prompting-fundamentals`)
- **Make every example obey the instructions exactly.** OpenAI's optimizer treats an example-instruction mismatch as a top failure mode, and its reasoning guide says: "Just ensure that the examples align very closely with your prompt instructions, as discrepancies between the two may produce poor results." (`openai-reasoning-best-practices`)
- **Keep every example in identical format: same tags, whitespace, newlines and separators.** Google found inconsistent example formatting produces unwanted output formats. (`google-gemini-prompt-design-strategies`)
- **Make example replies exactly as long as the real replies you want.** Examples set length more strongly than a length instruction: if they are paragraphs, the model writes paragraphs (AssemblyAI). (`assemblyai-voice-agent-prompting-guide`)
- **Show the concision you want instead of listing what not to do.** Anthropic's Opus 4.8 guide found positive examples more effective than negative examples or don't-instructions, with the line "Provide concise, focused responses. Skip non-essential context, and keep examples minimal." (`anthropic-prompting-opus-4-8`)
- **Fix a real failure with a Bad/Good pair quoted from a transcript, never with a rule for a case you only imagine.** The contrast teaches the rule better than a description, and speculative "what if the user asks about X" rules add noise. AssemblyAI's pair: don't say "This is a powerful tool that enables you to streamline your workflow...", say "It transcribes your calls and pulls out the key points." (`assemblyai-voice-agent-prompting-guide`)
- **For a judgment call, give the request, the right response and a one-line `<rationale>` saying what made it right.** The rationale teaches the principle instead of the surface; one complete example of this shape, with tool calls rendered as templated lines under your own tool's name, stopped Fable 5.1 copying source text unmarked into summaries (Anthropic, 2026-09-01): "CORRECT: The response is organized around where the two outlets agree and differ". Willison notes an example's content can still leak into outputs (the axolotl in Fable 5.1's consumer prompt), so vary them. (`anthropic-prompting-fable-5-1`, `willison-claude-fable-5-1-system-prompt`)
- **Put reasoning before the conclusion in examples and output schemas.** A conclusion emitted first can't use the reasoning after it, so OpenAI's meta-prompt reverses user examples that answer first: "Conclusion, classifications, or results should ALWAYS appear last." It matters most for non-reasoning models and structured outputs. (`openai-prompt-generation-metaprompts`)

## Examples for agents that use tools

- **Put the tool calls and the routing decision inside the example, not only the final reply.** Dialogflow CX references tools in examples for requests that use them and writes routing into the example's output ("Reroute back to the default playbook for further queries."); Vapi asks for the branch when a tool returns 0, 1 or many results. (`google-dialogflow-cx-playbook-best-practices`, `vapi-voice-prompting-guide`)
- **Write example tool calls in the model family's trained syntax, and pick the example set by model id.** An example in a foreign syntax teaches output the native parser won't recognize. Qwen Code keeps 4 example sets (qwen-coder, qwen-vl, gemma4, general), renders Gemma 4's calls as `<|tool_call>call:...{...}<tool_call|>`, and drops examples that mention tools not loaded this session (2026-10-15). (`qwen-code-system-prompts`)
- **If examples use made-up tools, say so right after them and list the real ones.** Without the warning the model calls the example tools. smolagents' CodeAgent system prompt spends 670 of its 1,380 words (word counts of the published file) on transcripts with notional tools, then says: "Above examples were using notional tools that might not exist for you." (`smolagents-prompt-templates`)
- **For how to use a tool, design expressive parameters instead of writing usage examples.** On the newest models, usage examples narrow what the model explores: Claude Code replaced a TodoWrite description of 9,100 characters (Anthropic's approximate count) of when-to-use lists and examples with one line, a status enum and one rule (Anthropic, 2026-07-24, Claude 5 models). Examples stay useful for output shape and judgment calls; tool description design is agent-harness, tools.md. (`anthropic-new-rules-context-engineering-claude-5`)

## Sample phrases in voice and chat

- **Give sample phrases with a variety rule, and whitelist the phrases that must stay verbatim for legal or brand reasons.** The realtime model follows sample phrases closely: before a variety rule, gpt-realtime confirmed with "Got it" on every turn. OpenAI's fix is a Variety section: "Do not repeat the same sentence twice." and "Vary your responses so it doesn't sound robotic." (`openai-realtime-prompting-guide`)
- **To get variation, describe the tone and give one or two example exchanges instead of a list of phrases.** AWS found Nova Sonic 2 more sensitive to phrase suggestions than Sonic 1, so listed fillers ("Well," "You know," "Actually,") get used very frequently; a list only suits fixed, on-brand phrasing, and Vapi warns that listed tokens get primed. (`aws-nova-sonic-2-voice-prompts`, `vapi-voice-prompting-guide`)
- **In a voice agent, include at least three example transcripts (a happy path, an edge case, an error recovery) as chat turns with their tool calls, and keep the set small.** Vapi and Hume call examples the main fix for unpredictable interpretation and for tone and pacing, Vapi asserts "Even 2–3 examples make a significant difference", and Hume warns that a long example library costs latency on every turn. (`vapi-voice-prompting-guide`, `hume-evi-prompting-guide`)

## How models read examples

- **Treat the conversation history as an example set: the model copies its own earlier turns.** Robinette et al. (Google DeepMind, EACL 2026 Findings) found prior turns' compliance mattered more than where the instruction sat; one non-compliant assistant turn dragged later compliance down, more so as conversations grew (10, 25 and 50 turns; Gemma 7B-it, Gemma2 27B-it, Llama3 8B-it, Llama3 70B-it). Seeded example turns and repaired histories are part of the prompt. (`google-verify-long-context-instruction-following`)
- **In repetitive work, vary the templates and phrasing of tool results so the transcript doesn't become a rut.** Manus watched an agent reviewing 20 resumes fall into a rhythm that caused drift, overgeneralization and hallucination; many near-identical examples in the prompt do the same. (`manus-context-engineering-lessons`)
- **Treat example order as a setting to tune on a dev set.** Li et al. (UC San Diego, 2025-11) found reordering the same examples moved accuracy as much as replacing them (mean difference under 1 percentage point; open models 0.5B-27B and GPT-5-nano, the most order-sensitive). Trying 16-32 orders recovered over 98% of the best accuracy for classification (99% at 128), and generation needed 64-100 for over 95%. (`ucsd-order-matters-icl`)
- **Measure a few example counts per model instead of assuming more is better.** Tang et al. (2026-07) found GPT-4o, GPT-3.5-turbo, LLaMA-3.1-8B and Gemma-3-4B peaked at 5 to 20 examples (the authors' approximate range) on requirement classification and then declined, DeepSeek-V3 plateaued, and LLaMA-3.2-3B broke above 20; TF-IDF-selected examples beat random ones, and the tuned count reached +1% over SOTA on PROMISE. Try counts such as 3, 5, 10 and 20. (`tang-few-shot-dilemma-over-prompting`)

## Worked reasoning in examples

- **Never paste full reasoning traces as examples into a reasoning model's prompt; state the method they carry as an instruction.** Wang et al. (2025-09-27) found accuracy fell with each added demo even when matched and high quality: on Qwen3 1.5B/3B/8B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill 7B/32B, AIME'25 dropped 6-16% with one demo (the authors' rough range) and up to 35% with three, GPQA-Diamond 1-5% at 1-shot and 4-8% at 3-shot. Turning demos into insights first (I2S) gained +14.0% on AIME'25 for GPT-4.1, and +2.7% AIME and +1.7% GPQA for o1-mini. (`wang-harm-to-help-reasoning-demos`)
- **Skip chain-of-thought exemplars on a strong instruct model too, and use examples only to fix output format.** Cheng et al. (2025-06) found zero-shot beat few-shot CoT on GSM8K and MATH once answer extraction was fixed, for Qwen2.5 0.5B-72B, LLaMA3, Gemma2 and Ministral; Qwen2.5-72B did as well with 50% noise in the exemplars, so the models largely ignored them while paying their tokens and latency. (`cheng-zero-shot-stronger-than-few-shot-cot`)

## Where the answer depends on the case

- **How many examples.** Anthropic recommends 3 to 5 (live docs, read 2026-10-22), and Tang et al. (2026-07) saw accuracy peak and then fall on smaller models (above). DeepMind (2024-10-17) measured significant gains from few-shot to many-shot (hundreds of examples, cached), comparable to fine-tuning and able to override pretraining biases that few-shot couldn't, with model-written rationales standing in for scarce human examples and cost growing linearly. On reasoning models, added reasoning demos cost accuracy instead: AIME'25 fell 6-16% with one demo (the authors' rough range) and up to 35% with three on Qwen3 1.5B/3B/8B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill 7B/32B (Wang et al., 2025-09-27; Worked reasoning in examples, above). Start at 3 to 5, reach for many-shot only when a classification or extraction behavior resists instructions and a handful of examples, and measure the count on your model. (`anthropic-prompting-best-practices`, `deepmind-many-shot-in-context-learning`, `tang-few-shot-dilemma-over-prompting`, `wang-harm-to-help-reasoning-demos`)
