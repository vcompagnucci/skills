# What the prompt holds

What goes into an agent's system prompt, in what order, and what stays out: the skeleton, the identity line, the goal and its finish line, the deployment context, and length. The prompt's format (XML or markdown, roles, templates) is structure.md; wording each line is instructions.md.

## Start from a skeleton

- **Give a support agent four sections: role and task, boundaries, safety, and response format.** Microsoft's and Google's sample support prompts both list allowed and prohibited topics and an exact fallback sentence, so behavior at the edge is predictable: "I don't have that information. Please contact support@contoso.com." (`microsoft-foundry-safety-system-messages`, `google-vertex-safety-system-instructions`)
- **For a complex agent, start from OpenAI's eight short sections in order: Role, Personality, Goal, Success criteria, Constraints, Tools, Output, Stop rules.** Outcome and stop conditions get their own sections instead of being buried in prose. Keep each section short and add detail only where it changes behavior; the last one reads "Stop rules: [when to retry, fallback, abstain, ask, or stop]". (`openai-gpt-5-6-prompt-guidance`)
- **Open with one line that states the task, with no heading above it.** The first line frames everything after it. OpenAI's Playground meta-prompt marks it "this should be the first line in the prompt, no section header", then adds details, optional Steps, an explicit Output Format with length and syntax, optional Examples with placeholders, and optional Notes for edge cases. Amazon's Nova template runs a persona line, then Model Instructions, Response Schema and Guardrails sections. (`openai-prompt-generation-metaprompts`, `amazon-nova-system-role`)
- **Give a coding agent's task prompt four parts: Goal, Context, Constraints, and Done when.** OpenAI says this keeps Codex scoped and makes it assume less; without "Done when" (tests passing, a bug no longer reproducing) the agent can't check its own work. (`openai-codex-best-practices`)
- **For a speech-to-speech agent, use short labeled sections and keep the greeting in its own field.** OpenAI's gpt-realtime-2 list is Role and Objective, Personality and Tone, Language, Reasoning, Message Channels, Preambles, Verbosity, Tools, Unclear Audio, Entity Capture, Long Context Behavior and Escalation; include only the ones your product needs. xAI's Grok Realtime template uses five H2s in second person ("Role & Persona", Objective, Conversation Flow, Guardrails & Escalation, Voice & Communication Style), a vendor template rather than a measured result. (`openai-prompting-realtime-models`, `xai-voice-prompting-guide`)

## Identity and role

- **Open with one identity sentence naming the product, the kind of agent and where it runs, and add the goal next.** Most published coding and browser prompts open with "You are <product>", and none spends more than two sentences on identity. Codex: "You are Codex, an agent based on GPT-6. You and the user share one workspace". Expert inflation is absent from the newest vendor-written prompts and survives in Kilo ("the best coding agent on the planet"), Roo and Copilot's older prompts. (`codex-prompt-gpt-6-family`, `opencode-per-provider-prompts`, `gemini-cli-system-prompt-modern`, `kilocode-per-provider-prompts`)
- **Use the role line for context, audience and scope, never to make the model smarter.** "You are a world-class expert in Physics..." raised GPQA accuracy reliably for none of 6 models, and on MMLU-Pro 5 of 6 showed no significant gain while low-knowledge personas hurt (Wharton, 2025-12, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, o3-mini, o4-mini, Gemini 2.0 and 2.5 Flash). Meta's and Amazon's guides claim role prompting improves accuracy but publish no measurement. Name the reader instead: Google shows the same task answered very differently for "college students" and for "primary school students". (`wharton-prompting-report-4-personas`, `meta-llama-prompt-engineering`, `amazon-nova-system-role`, `google-vertex-system-instructions-intro`)
- **State what a successful conversation ends with.** Deepgram says most prompts skip the win condition, which leaves the model nothing to measure itself against: "A successful call ends with the caller either having a confirmed appointment with provider name, date, time, and location stated back to them". (`deepgram-prompting-voice-agents`)
- **Define the role the way you would for a new hire.** Asana writes each agent's purpose, instructions and jobs "the way you would for a new hire's first quarter", and gives each agent a profile listing users, admins, skills, integrations and permissions. (`anthropic-asana-agents-you-can-coach`)
- **Keep personas close to the trained assistant (analyst, editor, consultant), not theatrical characters.** On Gemma 2 27B, Qwen 3 32B and Llama 3.3 70B, personas far from the Assistant complied with harmful requests at substantial rates while near ones rarely did, and therapy-like and AI-self-reflection conversations drifted away from it (Anthropic, 2026-01-19, 275 archetypes, not measured on Claude). Add stabilizing guidance for those contexts. (`anthropic-research-assistant-axis`)
- **For a persona that must stay in character, give its personality, background and quirks in detail, and treat any scenario list as examples of intent.** Anthropic's consistency page ("You are AcmeBot, the enterprise-grade AI assistant for AcmeTechCo.") pairs detail with canned replies; that page still carries legacy prefill content, and Anthropic's newer app prompts give principles with their reasons instead of scripted lines. Tone and persona voice are voice-and-output.md; examples are examples.md. (`anthropic-increase-consistency`)
- **Name the model in one line when the app must identify it correctly.** Without it the model may misname itself or write an outdated model string into generated code: "The assistant is Claude, created by Anthropic. The current model is Claude Opus 5.5." (`anthropic-prompting-best-practices`)

## The goal and the finish line

- **State the goal as the outcome and its completion bar.** OpenAI's GPT-5.6 support example: "Resolve the customer's issue end to end." Success means deciding eligibility from policy and account evidence, completing any allowed action before responding, and "if required evidence is missing, ask for the smallest missing field". GPT-5.6 picks efficient paths when it knows what good looks like, and prescribed steps can lock it into a worse one; how much path to prescribe is procedures.md. (`openai-gpt-5-6-prompt-guidance`)
- **When trimming, keep five things.** OpenAI found these still change behavior on GPT-5.6: the user-visible outcome; success criteria and stopping conditions; safety, business, evidence and permission constraints; tool-routing rules where the route depends on context; and the required output shape and validation. Everything else is a candidate for removal. (`openai-gpt-5-6-prompt-guidance`)

## Deployment context

- **Tell the agent the channel, its conditions, and who usually contacts it, and tie each to a behavior.** A phone agent must confirm spellings harder than a web widget because mobile audio drops consonants. Deepgram's line: "This is a phone call over a mobile connection. Audio quality will vary." then "When names or spellings are unclear, ask the caller to repeat rather than guessing." (`deepgram-prompting-voice-agents`)
- **Give a support agent a glossary of the business's own terms as its own section.** Product names, plan tiers and internal jargon sit apart from policies and knowledge articles; Sierra lists the glossary as one of eight standard context block types. (`sierra-context-engineering-key-to-great-agents`)
- **Tell the model what the user can't see.** The model assumes the user sees what it sees, so when your UI hides tool output it runs commands to "show" output nobody sees and leaves needed content out of its reply. Anthropic's fix, after each hidden result: "Only you see that command's output" (Fable 5.1, 2026-09-01). (`anthropic-prompting-fable-5-1`)
- **State environment facts that break the model's assumptions.** Models trained on interactive shells chain `cd` in one call and rely on it in the next; mini-swe-agent says "Directory or environment variable changes are not persistent. Every action is executed in a new subshell." (`mini-swe-agent-prompts`)
- **On the API, write the date, the model identity and your formatting defaults yourself.** Anthropic's app system prompts supply these, and they don't apply to the API. (`anthropic-system-prompts-overview`)
- **Give a search-capable agent today's date and the model's knowledge cutoff, rendered once at conversation start.** Without the date, Haiku 5.5 grounded its answers less in recent search results (Anthropic, 2026-10-07): "The current date is {{current_date}}." Google lists a knowledge cutoff as additional context a system instruction can provide (2026-10-21). On a later day, send the new date in the newest turn rather than editing the system prompt; OpenAI's GPT-5.5 and later are the exception (models.md "GPT"), and handling post-cutoff claims is grounding.md. (`anthropic-prompting-haiku-5-5`, `google-vertex-use-system-instructions`)
- **Give rich references as inputs.** Code or an HTML mockup beats a prose description or a screenshot. (`anthropic-new-rules-context-engineering-claude-5`)

## What stays out, and where it goes

- **Spend your effort on the system prompt, and give it only what a third or more of traffic needs.** Anthropic calls the system prompt product context, "where you should spend a lot of time" if you build your own harness, and its commerce guide puts anything relevant to "a third or more of your traffic" in the prompt and the rest in skills. Loading the long tail on demand is agent-harness, context.md. (`anthropic-new-rules-context-engineering-claude-5`, `anthropic-commerce-agents`)
- **Sort each instruction into its home.** OpenAI's cross-vendor migration guide keeps the agent role, task policy, tool-use rules and response format in the agent's instructions, repo conventions and test commands in AGENTS.md, reusable procedures in skills, templates in skill assets, and permission enforcement in guardrails or approvals (2026-05-07). Reference material and scripts stay out of the main prompt, and longer task instructions live in workspace files. (`openai-migrate-from-claude-agent-sdk`)
- **Put what must be guaranteed in a hook, not in prose.** Anthropic's split for Claude Code: what the agent should know goes in the instruction file, how it responds in the output style, and what must be guaranteed in a hook, because prose is followed probabilistically. (`anthropic-claude-code-output-styles`)
- **Keep procedures apart from tone and style.** Zendesk separates procedures (what to do) from communication guidelines, and Intercom Fin separates its guidance categories, so one can be edited without touching the other (2026-10-13). (`zendesk-auto-assist-procedures`, `intercom-fin-guidance-best-practices`)
- **Give the agent only the details the task needs.** What isn't in the prompt can't leak, and "Extra content reduces Claude's adherence" to no-leak instructions (Anthropic). What must never be in the prompt is agent-security, conversation.md. (`anthropic-reduce-prompt-leak`)
- **In a voice agent, keep the greeting, history, function schemas, model, voice, rate and language selection in platform configuration.** Deepgram calls controlling these from the prompt the most common prompt bug: a configured greeting is spoken directly by TTS, and history rebuilt in the prompt duplicates context. The prompt says when to call a function and the schema says how; Deepgram's managed-LLM prompt cap is 25,000 characters. (`deepgram-prompting-voice-agents`)
- **Choose the SDK's base prompt on purpose.** In Anthropic's Agent SDK the default is a minimal prompt that "omits the rest of the `claude_code` preset's content, including its security and safety instructions"; the `claude_code` preset with an append keeps them, and a custom string holds only what you write. Replacing Claude Code's prompt with an output style drops its engineering instructions (scope, comments, verification) unless the style sets `keep-coding-instructions: true`. (`anthropic-agent-sdk-modifying-system-prompts`, `anthropic-claude-code-output-styles`)
- **Make every section beat its own absence.** LangChain's Deep Agents found no prompt variant in its internal experiments "statistically distinguishable" from the others and shipped an empty default base prompt (2026-07-22, data unpublished): "parsimony argues for the leanest agent". It cut prose that restated tool schemas and kept skills, memory and path routing text because those carry information the schemas don't. (`langchain-deepagents-prompts`)

## Length

- **Decide where each piece of guidance lives before deciding length.** Short prompts are short because the guidance lives elsewhere: mini-swe-agent's 16-word system prompt puts everything in the first user message. Word counts per model (browser-use, Codex, OpenCode) are in models.md, "Running several model families", "GPT" and "Open-weight and small local models". (`mini-swe-agent-prompts`, `browser-use-system-prompts`, `opencode-per-provider-prompts`, `codex-prompt-gpt-6-family`)
- **Keep an agent prompt well under a few thousand words, and split the rest into subagents or loaded procedures.** On 707 instructions from 50 real agent apps, AgentIF found success near zero for every model once instructions passed 6,000 words; the best model, o1-mini, met 59.8% of constraints (Tsinghua, 2025-05). Rule counts are rule-sets.md. (`tsinghua-agentif-benchmark`)
- **On a small model, shorten a long prompt before adding nudges.** Haiku 5.5 with a long coding-agent prompt at low effort stopped early and skipped searches, while with a short prompt the date alone sufficed; moving from low to medium effort "roughly halved early stopping" and "more than doubled the output tokens for each attempt" (Anthropic, 2026-10-07). (`anthropic-prompting-haiku-5-5`)
