# Wording one instruction

How to word a single line of an agent's prompt so it changes what the agent does: its reason, its positive target, its scope, its number, and emphasis kept for invariants. Customer-support examples come first where one exists. Many rules at once (conflicts, how many a model keeps, repetition, order in a list) are in rule-sets.md.

## Say what you want and why

- **Write each support rule as one second-person if-then with a concrete case.** Intercom says "When customers ask about pricing, make sure Fin answers correctly." fails, and a single-purpose rule works: "If a customer asks about the 'search' feature, first ask which product they are using before responding." Address the model as "you" ("You are an AI assistant that...") rather than describing it in the third person, as Microsoft's guidance recommends. (`intercom-fin-guidance-best-practices`, `microsoft-foundry-safety-system-messages`)
- **Say what the agent does when a request is ambiguous, out of scope, or beyond what it knows.** Microsoft lists missing fallbacks as a main cause of invented facts and inconsistent behavior. Its line: "If you don't have enough information to answer, ask a clarifying question. If you still can't answer, say you don't know." (`microsoft-foundry-system-message-design`)
- **Give the reason with the rule.** The model generalizes from the reason to cases the rule never named; a bare command gives it nothing to generalize from. Anthropic's example: "Your response will be read aloud by a text-to-speech engine, so never use ellipses since the text-to-speech engine will not know how to pronounce them." It also says "NEVER use bullet points" works worse than the preference plus its reason. (`anthropic-prompting-best-practices`, `anthropic-prompt-engineering-best-practices-2026-blog`)
- **Explain the cost model instead of writing "be efficient".** A bare efficiency rule makes the agent truncate reads and then spend turns recovering. Gemini CLI tells the model the whole history is resent every turn, says "Unnecessary turns are generally more expensive than other types of wasted context", and ends with "Efficiency is an important, but secondary concern." (`gemini-cli-system-prompt-modern`)
- **Write the target behavior, never only the banned one.** "Your response should be composed of smoothly flowing prose paragraphs." steers more reliably than "Do not use markdown", and the prompt's own formatting pulls the reply: less markdown in the prompt, less in the output. (`anthropic-prompting-best-practices`)
- **Replace a long banlist with one short positive principle ("do not output phone numbers").** Every banned phrase sits in context as an active token, so the list becomes a menu of likely outputs (Vapi). Rana measured the pull on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (2026-01): violations rose from 9% at pressure 0.1 to over 46% at 0.9, and 87.5% of failures attended to the named word more than to the negation. On 1-4B open models, Elkins and Chun saw an action endorsed 24% of the time when asked affirmatively and 77% under simple negation (2026-07-08). (`vapi-voice-prompting-guide`, `rana-semantic-gravity-wells-negative-constraints`, `elkins-negation-sensitivity-audit`)
- **Word a task as the result and the bar, and let the model pick the path.** OpenAI's current ChatGPT and Codex doc says step lists remove the model's room to search, compare and adjust: "Turn these meeting notes into a short update for the project team. Put the decisions and next steps first." "Highly prescriptive prompting degraded results" in OpenAI's data agent, and Anthropic's Claude 5 prompt swapped "default to writing no comments" for "Write code that reads like the surrounding code: match its comment density, naming, and idiom." Describe a process only when the process itself matters. (`openai-codex-prompting-doc`, `openai-data-agent`, `anthropic-new-rules-context-engineering-claude-5`)
- **State the larger task, who it is for and what the output enables, before the request.** Without intent the model guesses the goal and can't connect the task to what it knows; Anthropic's template is "I'm working on [the larger task] for [who it's for]. They need [what the output enables]." Then read the brief as a new intern would and add what they'd trip on: where to start, how you want it done, which docs are current. Cognition calls this defensive prompting. (`anthropic-prompting-fable-5`, `cognition-coding-agents-101`)
- **Bound the job in one sentence when it could grow without end.** Without a boundary, agents chase every instance or the hardest case first. OpenAI's example: "Your job is to find clear fixes to simple issues, not to exhaustively fix every flaky test." Give each prompt one deliverable, because "If you can't describe "good" in one or two sentences, the model can't reliably hit it either." (`openai-maintaining-software-gpt-6`, `openai-chatgpt-enterprise-prompting-guide`)

## Be specific where the default is wrong

- **Put a number on every limit.** A count lets the model check itself; "don't loop" gives it nothing to test. Copilot's Kimi prompt says "Never call the same tool with the same arguments more than twice in a row." Zed allows 1-2 focused attempts at fixing diagnostics, Codex gives a simple multiple-choice question 60 seconds before it proceeds on a stated assumption, Gemini CLI asks for fewer than 3 lines of text, and mini-swe-agent truncates output above 10,000 characters; browser-use's step-budget checkpoint is in progress-and-stopping.md "Context and step budgets". (`github-copilot-vscode-agent-prompts`, `zed-agent-system-prompts`, `browser-use-system-prompts`, `codex-prompt-gpt-6-family`, `gemini-cli-system-prompt-modern`, `mini-swe-agent-prompts`)
- **Write length and style as checks the model can run on its draft.** "Short" and "detailed" leave the model on its default. Cohere asks for "between 3 and 5 sentences" or 250-300 words, and AssemblyAI found abstract style rules don't land in voice agents while "If your reply is more than 15 words, shorten it." does. (`cohere-crafting-effective-prompts`, `assemblyai-voice-agent-prompting-guide`)
- **Constrain taste with numbers and named failure looks, never adjectives.** OpenAI's GPT-5.5 frontend block says "Cards are kept at 8px border radius or less unless the existing design system requires otherwise." Its frontend guide puts hard constraints first and calls real copy "one of the simplest ways" to improve results, because underspecified prompts fall back to common training patterns. Constrain the result, not the path: OpenAI's Astra post says "overly specific guidance can now hinder results where it previously helped" when it prescribes steps. (`openai-frontend-prompt-instructions`, `openai-frontends-gpt-5-4`, `openai-rethinking-skills-prompts-gpt-6-astra`)
- **Lead with one principle, and list named patterns only when the principle swaps one default for another.** "Lead with the outcome." matched an enumerated list of verbose behaviors on Fable 5 (Anthropic, 2026-06-09). For design, "avoid a generic look" moved Opus 5.5 to its next default, so Anthropic names the patterns ("Do not use a cream or off-white background, italic accent words in headlines") and extends the list after the first output (2026-09-22). (`anthropic-prompting-fable-5`, `anthropic-prompting-opus-5-5`, `anthropic-getting-the-most-out-of-opus-5-5`)
- **Write a spec when you need the same result every run.** With a detailed spec, models and effort levels built much more similar things; underspecified tasks varied widely, and Opus 5.5 assumed more at higher effort (Anthropic, 2026-09-25). Write down every requirement you rely on, even ones the model meets today: CMU found unstated requirements "2x as likely to regress across model or prompt changes", with accuracy 22.6% lower on average (2025-05, gpt-4o, Llama-3.3-70B, o3-mini). (`anthropic-spending-your-effort`, `cmu-prompt-underspecification`)
- **Give the whole spec in the first turn.** The same information spread over several turns lost 39% on average (o3 fell from 98.1 to 64.1), because early wrong attempts stay in context (Microsoft and Salesforce, via Breunig, 2025-06). When a conversation has drifted, restart with one consolidated prompt. (`breunig-how-contexts-fail`)
- **Give a test plus a default for the unsure case.** A stated bar and tie-breaker keep output short and consistent: "An item earns a line when the reader would act on it today" and "When unsure, leave it out." (Anthropic, 2026-10-08). In a finding step, ask for every issue with its confidence and severity and filter in a separate pass: told "only report high-severity issues" or "be conservative", Opus 5 follows the bar literally and reports less (2026-07-24). (`anthropic-building-effective-agent-automations`, `anthropic-prompting-opus-5`)
- **Aim a rule at the cases it is for.** A blanket "search for any present-day factual question" made Haiku 5.5 search on half of no-search prompts with no more correct answers. A targeted stale-knowledge line raised search where answers had changed with 0-3% extra searches elsewhere, closing with "Facts that can't change need no search." (Anthropic, 2026-10-07). (`anthropic-prompting-haiku-5-5`)
- **Start minimal and add a line only for a failure you saw.** Anthropic's right altitude sits between brittle if-else logic and vague guidance: start with a minimal prompt on the best model, add instructions and examples for observed failures, and remember "Minimal does not necessarily mean short". Keep a gotchas section built from real failures, the highest-signal part: "The subscriptions table is append-only." (`anthropic-effective-context-engineering`, `anthropic-how-we-use-skills`)
- **When the agent fails, rewrite the task line to say what done means.** One "do not" per observed failure piles up without fixing the missing requirement, and each extra line makes the rest slightly less followed. Retell's rewrite: "Help the caller choose a replacement delivery date, save the change, and confirm the updated date with them." (`retell-general-prompting-principles`)

## Scope every rule

- **Open a conditional rule with when it applies, and make the trigger checkable.** AgentIF found over 30% of conditional-rule errors came from the model misjudging whether the condition held, so write a trigger the model can check (for example, a rule that opens with the customer mentioning a chargeback) and keep such rules few (Tsinghua, 2025-05). Anthropic's Sonnet 5.5 prompt opens a rule with "This applies only when the person explicitly says the medicine is for a child". (`tsinghua-agentif-benchmark`, `anthropic-system-prompt-sonnet-5-5`)
- **Name every case a rule covers.** Current models apply a rule only where it was stated: on gpt-realtime-2 a rule about "a confirmation code" never fired for an order ID, so OpenAI's rule lists "confirmation codes, order IDs, ticket IDs, reset PINs, claim numbers, tracking numbers, or account numbers". Sonnet 5 needs "Apply this formatting to every section, not just the first one", and a confirmation rule scoped to "write actions that modify user data" beats "Always ask for confirmation before doing anything", which added confirmations before harmless lookups. (`openai-prompting-realtime-models`, `anthropic-prompting-sonnet-5`)
- **Write conditions in words and compute counts in code.** OpenAI's realtime guide found words more reliable: "IF MORE THAN THREE FAILURES THEN ESCALATE", not "IF x > 3 THEN ESCALATE". Models count characters, words and items badly, so Mistral passes the count in as input instead of writing "if the record is longer than 100 characters". (`openai-realtime-prompting-guide`, `mistral-prompting-capabilities`)
- **On a non-reasoning model, write a check as a condition plus an action.** It has no hidden step for a silent check, so it skips the check or says it aloud to the caller. Vapi names the conditions and ends with the action: "politely decline or end the call." (`vapi-voice-prompting-guide`)
- **Tell the agent to treat the user's own exclusions as hard constraints.** An agent chasing completion drifts past "skip" or "only X" in the request. browser-use's prompt for its own model says instructions containing those words "are hard constraints. Before each action, check: does this violate any constraint?" (`browser-use-system-prompts`)
- **Word durable rules as outcomes, with one term per concept and no dated facts.** A rule naming a function stops matching after a rename, so OpenAI's code-review rules say "Describe outcomes, not function names that may change." and prune rules that keep producing noise. Dated facts go wrong silently and synonyms make the model wonder whether two things differ, so Anthropic keeps time-sensitive information out or fences it in an "old patterns" section. (`openai-codex-code-review-rules`, `anthropic-skill-authoring-best-practices`)
- **Test hardest the rules that cut against trained habits.** On 1,012 counter-intuitive instructions (no bullet points, no code comments, a deliberate flaw), models that top standard benchmarks dropped (English overall scores): o3-high 75.66, GPT-5-high 73.72, Claude 4 Opus Thinking 67.16, Qwen3-235B Thinking 54.22 against Instruct 40.28 (Inverse IFEval, 2025-09-04). Give such a rule its reason and a positive description of the output, and prefer a thinking mode for it. (`bytedance-inverse-ifeval`)

## Keep emphasis for invariants

- **Keep capitals and absolute words for one or two true invariants, and write judgment calls as decision rules.** OpenAI's GPT-5.6 guidance: "Use ALWAYS, NEVER, must, and only for true invariants such as safety rules, required fields, or actions that should never happen." Overused, they make the model rigid, overcautious or unable to handle reasonable exceptions; OpenAI's Realtime cookbook advised capitalizing key rules on 2026-02-25, and its newer gpt-realtime-2 guide reversed that. In a support prompt, Zendesk still recommends one: "VERIFY CUSTOMER IDENTITY BEFORE DISCLOSING ACCOUNT INFORMATION" (2026-08-05). (`openai-gpt-5-6-prompt-guidance`, `openai-realtime-prompting-guide`, `openai-prompting-realtime-models`, `zendesk-generative-procedures-best-practices`)
- **Drop CRITICAL and MUST from a tool's trigger line: write "Use this tool when...".** Anthropic found "CRITICAL: You MUST use this tool when..." overtriggers on newer Claude models: Opus 4.5 and Opus 4.6 are "more responsive to the system prompt than previous models", so prompts written against tool or skill undertriggering now overtrigger (docs, read 2026-10-22). That evidence covers tool and skill trigger lines only. (`anthropic-prompting-best-practices`)
- **Enforce every invariant in code as well, and stop adding emphasis once a rule keeps breaking.** The identity check itself belongs in code (agent-security, conversation.md). When a voice agent keeps breaking a rule already marked "ABSOLUTELY NEVER", Deepgram enforces it in server code: output gates for silence, suppression of text emitted before tool calls. (`zendesk-generative-procedures-best-practices`, `deepgram-prompting-voice-agents`)
- **Mark one line that keeps being skipped with "IMPORTANT", never many.** When many lines are emphasized, none stands out (Anthropic's Claude Code docs). Uppercase draws a non-reasoning model's attention without reliably improving the answer: Dillitzer et al. saw the attention shift in every non-reasoning model of 13 tested, and on their discovery model alternating case cost up to 2.88 points while an uppercase target gained 1.85 (2026-08-04). Casing on reasoning models, measured by Dillitzer et al. on Qwen3 thinking models only, is in models.md "Settings before wording". (`anthropic-claude-code-best-practices-docs`, `esslingen-attention-is-case-sensitive`)
- **Replace emphasis lists and thoroughness boosters written for weaker models with a plain description.** Cursor swapped "DO NOT", "You must" and "Important" lists for a description of how each tool behaves, cut 66% of its system prompt by its own estimate, and lowered token cost 7% with no quality drop across model families (2026-09-23). Cursor's "Be THOROUGH when gathering information." block made GPT-5 search repeatedly when its own knowledge sufficed (OpenAI, 2025-08-07). Confirm each removal with an A/B test. (`cursor-improved-token-efficiency`, `openai-gpt-5-prompting-guide`)
- **State precedence plainly instead of adding tips, threats, politeness or authority framing.** Wharton found no significant aggregate effect from tips or threats on GPQA and MMLU-Pro, while the wording swung single questions both ways (2025-08). Geng et al. measured 13 framing strategies, such as "This is urgent" or "As your supervisor", on five open models: reciprocity, authority claims and direct override commands moved which directive won most, in the same ranking across models (2026-09-11); attackers pull the same lever (agent-security, conversation.md). (`wharton-prompting-report-3-tips-threats`, `geng-pragmatic-influence-instructions`)

## Write limits and refusals precisely

- **Keep what the agent may promise as a boundary inside the steps.** Gorgias writes handling as positive steps and keeps limits such as "don't commit to a refund before a returned item arrives" as boundaries; a bare "don't reply to complaints" gives the agent no action. (`gorgias-best-practices-writing-skills`)
- **Ban an artifact by its general class, and never tell a model not to think.** "Do not include internal or system XML tags in your response." works better than naming the tag, and rules against thinking increased tag leakage with thinking disabled (Anthropic, Opus 5, 2026-07-24). (`anthropic-prompting-opus-5`)
- **Keep a list of banned phrases short, and back must-never strings with an output filter.** AssemblyAI lists exact strings (Never say "certainly", "absolutely", "happy to help", or "great question".) because the model matches concrete strings better than "be casual", against Vapi's priming warning above. Neither published a measurement, so the first-party default holds: say what to do, name concrete patterns only after a principle fails, and test whether a banned phrase rises once you add it. (`assemblyai-voice-agent-prompting-guide`, `vapi-voice-prompting-guide`, `rana-semantic-gravity-wells-negative-constraints`)
- **When the agent declines, have it state the principle, never the detection mechanics.** Narrating the boundary teaches users how to reframe around it. Anthropic's Opus 5.5 prompt says Claude "states the principle rather than the detection mechanics", in its reasoning as well as its reply (2026-09-22). (`anthropic-system-prompt-opus-5-5`, `anthropic-system-prompt-opus-5`)
- **Say how long a refusal lasts and what to offer instead.** Without it, splitting a request across turns gets around the rule. Claude's consumer prompt: "it keeps declining narrower or reworded versions of it for the rest of that conversation, and offers to describe or analyze the work instead" (2026-09-02). (`willison-claude-fable-5-1-system-prompt`)

## Delete lines that change nothing

- **Delete "think carefully before answering" from a chat prompt.** Opus 5.5 sets its own thinking from effort, and removing the line made replies start sooner with no clear quality decline in Anthropic's chat-product test (2026-09-22). Give a "think step by step" line the same check, but the verdict turns on whether reasoning is on: Wharton measured it on o3-mini, o4-mini and Gemini 2.5 Flash with reasoning, and on Gemini 2.0 Flash and Sonnet 3.5 without (2025-06-08), in models.md "Settings before wording", which also covers effort versus wording. (`anthropic-prompting-opus-5-5`, `wharton-prompting-report-2-chain-of-thought`)
- **Point every tool or knowledge instruction at something configured.** The agent can't act on an instruction to search a FAQ that isn't one of its knowledge sources, and the line misleads whoever maintains the prompt (Microsoft, 2026-07-14). (`microsoft-copilot-studio-instructions-guidance`)
