# Widgets, Live Activities, and notifications

How an app reaches people outside its own windows on iPhone, iPad, and Mac, through widgets, Live Activities, and notifications. For Apple, each surface is a glance or a quick action from the app's main purpose, always current, safe to show on a Lock Screen, and never a bare launcher, an ad, or an interruption people didn't ask for. (`widgets`, `live-activities`, `notifications`, `managing-notifications`)

**Contents:** Choosing the surface · Widgets · Live Activities · Notifications · Key source articles

## Choosing the surface

- **Use a widget for content that changes through the day, and a Live Activity for a task with a clear start and end.** Widgets never update in real time, so track a delivery or match of up to eight hours in a Live Activity. (`widgets`, `live-activities`)

## Widgets (`widgets`)

- **Weigh each widget's size, context, and rendering mode, and consider a custom design per context rather than the defaults.** Widgets adapt to people's customization and to where they appear: the iPhone and iPad Home Screen and Lock Screen, and the Mac desktop and Notification Center. (`widgets`)
- **System family widgets come in small, medium, large, and extra large, and can include interactive elements.** On iPhone, small, medium, and large go on the Home Screen and Today View, small also appears in StandBy, and extra large isn't supported. (`widgets`)
- **(iPadOS, macOS) iPad and Mac support all four system family sizes.** iPad places them on the Home Screen and Today View, with small also on the Lock Screen, and Mac places them on the desktop and in Notification Center. (`widgets`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Make a Lock Screen widget show useful information instead of acting as another way to launch your app.** Accessory widgets there hold very little information and come in three shapes: inline text above the clock, and circular and rectangular shapes below it. (`widgets`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Design for the four Home Screen appearances people choose: light, dark, clear, and tinted.** Light and dark show your full-color design. Clear desaturates the widget and adds translucency, highlights, and Liquid Glass, and tinted desaturates it and applies the person's tint color. (`widgets`)
- **Expect full-color rendering on the iPhone and iPad Home Screen and Today View, in iPhone StandBy, and on the Mac desktop and in Notification Center.** It doesn't change the color of your views, though StandBy removes the background. (`widgets`)
- **Expect vibrant rendering on the iPhone and iPad Lock Screen, in iPhone StandBy in low light, and on the Mac desktop.** It desaturates text, images, and gauges and colors content for the Lock Screen or desktop background. People can tint the Lock Screen. (`widgets`)
- **Choose a simple idea tied to your app's main purpose, with timely content and relevant functionality.** People who use Weather are often most interested in current conditions and the high and low temperatures, so the Weather widgets prioritize them. (`widgets`)
- **Give quick access to meaningful content, useful actions, and deep links to key areas of the app.** A widget that's just a copy of the app icon adds little, and people are less likely to keep it. (`widgets`)
- **Prefer dynamic information that changes throughout the day.** People may not keep a widget in a prominent position if its content never seems to change, so although widgets don't update minute to minute, find ways to keep content fresh. (`widgets`)
- **Look for opportunities to surprise and delight.** For example, a calendar widget might get a unique visual treatment on meaningful occasions, like birthdays or holidays. (`widgets`)
- **Offer more sizes only when they add value, and don't stretch a small widget's content to fill a larger one.** Small widgets typically show one piece of information and larger ones add layers and actions. One widget in its best size beats offering all sizes. (`widgets`)
- **Balance information density so essentials read at a glance and details reward a longer look.** Sparse layouts make a widget seem unnecessary and dense ones are less glanceable. If a layout is too dense, consider a larger size or graphics in place of text. (`widgets`)
- **Show only information directly related to the widget's main purpose, even in larger sizes.** Larger widgets can show more data or richer visualizations, but every Calendar widget stays centered on upcoming events while the range of information grows with the size. (`widgets`)
- **Use brand colors, typefaces, and stylized glyphs to make a widget recognizable, without overpowering information or looking out of place.** With them, people seldom need your logo or app icon. For content from multiple sources, a small top-right logo is sufficient. (`widgets`)
- **Decide whether the widget shows content automatically or lets people customize it.** Some widgets need configuring to show what's most useful to each person, like Stocks, where people select the stocks to track, while Podcasts shows recent content automatically, so it needs no setup. (`widgets`)
- **Avoid mirroring your widget's appearance inside your app.** An in-app element that looks like the widget but doesn't behave like it can confuse people, and they may not try other ways to interact with it because they expect widget behavior. (`widgets`)
- **Let people know when signing in adds value.** If your widget offers more when people are signed in to your app, say so: an app that shows upcoming reservations might display "Sign in to view reservations" to people who are signed out. (`widgets`)
- **Keep your widget up to date at a frequency that fits its data and when people need it.** A beach tide widget is useful updating hourly, though conditions change constantly. If people check more often than you can update, show the last update time. (`widgets`)
- **Let the system refresh dates and times, and show content quickly instead of hiding stale data behind placeholders.** Widgets refresh periodically but not in real time, within limits the system may adjust, so automatic date and time refreshes preserve your update opportunities. (`widgets`)
- **Use animated transitions of up to two seconds to bring attention to data updates.** Many SwiftUI views animate content updates by default, and standard or custom animations tell people new information is available or content displays differently. (`widgets`)
- **Offer simple functionality directly related to the widget's content, and reserve complexity for your app.** Buttons and toggles complete tasks without launching the app, like the Reminders toggles that mark a task as completed, and interacting anywhere else opens the app. (`widgets`)
- **Open your app at the right location when people tap or click the widget.** Deep link to details and actions related to its content instead of making people navigate: a medium Stocks widget opens Stocks to a page about the symbol. (`widgets`)
- **Keep an interactive widget glanceable and uncluttered, and avoid app-like layouts.** Several links, buttons, and toggles can make sense, but size targets so people tap or click them confidently without unintended interactions. Inline accessory widgets offer only one tap target. (`widgets`)
- **Supply content at appropriate sizes, guided by the specifications and Apple Design Resources, and let the system scale it.** iOS resizes content designed for large devices to fit small ones, and iPadOS renders widgets large, then scales them down for the Home Screen. (`widgets`)
- **Use the standard 16 pt margin for most widgets, or 11 pt for tighter groupings of graphics, buttons, or background shapes.** Standard margins keep edges from crowding and looking cluttered. Widgets use smaller margins on the Mac desktop and the Lock Screen, including StandBy. (`widgets`)
- **Coordinate your content's corner radius with the widget's.** A SwiftUI container applies the correct radius so content looks good inside the widget's rounded corners. (`widgets`)
- **Prefer the system font, text styles, and SF Symbols.** The system font looks at home on any platform and comes in many weights, styles, and sizes, and SF Symbols align and scale with it. (`widgets`)
- **Use a custom font sparingly, and make sure it's easy to read at a glance.** It often works well for a widget's large text, with SF Pro for the smaller text. (`widgets`)
- **Keep text at 11 pt or larger, and don't rasterize it.** Smaller text is too hard for many people to read, and real text elements and styles scale well and let VoiceOver speak your content. (`widgets`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Choose fonts through SwiftUI so widget text supports Dynamic Type sizes from Large to AX5.** That works for the system font and for custom fonts. (`widgets`)
- **Use color in a widget without letting it compete with the content.** Beautiful colors draw the eye but work best when people can still absorb the information at a glance. Your asset catalog can also set the colors of the widget's editing-mode interface. (`widgets`)
- **Convey meaning without relying on specific colors.** Widgets can appear monochromatic, with or without a custom tint color, so express meaning with text and iconography as well as color. (`widgets`)
- **Use full-color images judiciously, and consider reserving them for media content like album art.** Tinted and clear appearances desaturate images by default. Keeping full color draws special attention and can look out of place, so make such images smaller than the widget. (`widgets`)
- **Support light and dark appearances in full-color rendering.** Prefer light backgrounds in the light appearance and dark ones in the dark, and consider semantic system colors for text and backgrounds so they adapt, or color variants in your asset catalog. (`widgets`)
- **Group widget components into accent and primary groups for accented rendering.** On the iPhone and iPad Home Screen and Today View, it swaps your background for a tint effect or Liquid Glass, and on iPhone, iPad, and Mac it tints both groups white. (`widgets`)
- **Offer enough contrast to keep vibrant widgets legible.** Pixel opacity sets the strength of the blurred background material, with fully transparent pixels letting it through as is, and pixel brightness sets how vibrant content looks: brighter grays give more contrast, darker ones less. (`widgets`)
- **Create optimized assets for the best vibrant effect.** Render images, numbers, and text at full opacity, with white or light gray for the most prominent content and darker grays for secondary elements. Confirm grayscale contrast, and use opaque grays, not opacities of white. (`widgets`)
- **Design a realistic gallery preview that shows what each widget type and size can do.** It helps people make an informed decision. Show real data, or realistic simulated data if real data takes too long to generate or load. (`widgets`)
- **Design placeholder content that keeps your widget recognizable while its data loads.** Combine static interface components with semi-opaque shapes for dynamic content: rectangles of different widths for lines of text, and circles or squares for glyphs and images. (`widgets`)
- **Write a succinct widget description that starts with an action verb.** The gallery shows it to explain what the widget does, as in "Keep track of your upcoming events and meetings." Skip phrases like "This widget shows…" and use approachable language with sentence-style capitalization. (`widgets`)
- **Group your widget's sizes together, with a single description.** Grouped, people don't mistake each size for a different widget, and one description avoids repetition while showing how each size offers a slightly different perspective on the same content. (`widgets`)
- **Consider coloring the Add button with your brand color.** It appears below your group of widgets after people choose your app in the widget gallery, and color reminds them of your brand, like Notes' yellow or Weather's blue. (`widgets`)
- **(iOS) Support the Always-On display on iPhone.** It renders Lock Screen widgets with reduced luminance, so use levels of gray that keep enough contrast and your content legible. (`widgets`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Offer a Live Activity, not a widget, for real-time updates.** Widgets don't show real-time information, so a task or event people track for a limited time with frequent updates suits one. They share frameworks and design, so consider developing both in tandem. (`widgets`)
- **(iOS) Expect StandBy to show two small widgets side by side, scaled up to fill the Lock Screen with their backgrounds removed.** In low light, the system renders them in a monochromatic look with a red tint. (`widgets`)
- **(iOS) In StandBy, limit rich images and color for meaning, and use no background color.** Scale up and rearrange text so people can read the widget from a greater distance, and let it blend into the black background. (`widgets`)
- **(iOS) Size iPhone system family widgets for screens from 320x568 pt to 430x932 pt.** Across that range, small grows from 141x141 to 170x170 pt, medium from 292x141 to 364x170 pt, and large from 292x311 to 364x382 pt. (`widgets`)
- **(iOS) On iPhone, accessory widgets are smallest on a 375x667 pt screen and largest on 430x932 pt, and a 320x568 pt screen has none.** Circular runs from 68x68 to 76x76 pt, rectangular from 153x68 to 172x76 pt, and inline from 225x26 to 257x26 pt. (`widgets`)
- **(iPadOS) iPadOS renders widgets at a canvas size, then scales them down to a device size.** On a 1024x1366 pt iPad, small goes from 170x170 to 160x160 pt and extra large from 795x378.5 to 748x356 pt, and on 768x1024 pt, small from 141x141 to 120x120 pt. (`widgets`)

## Live Activities (`live-activities`)

- **Offer Live Activities for tasks and events with a defined beginning and end, lasting no more than eight hours.** They work best for short to medium activities, like a food delivery, a soccer match, or a workout people can pause or cancel. (`live-activities`)
- **Expect a Live Activity to start on iPhone or iPad and appear across the system automatically.** It shows on the Lock Screen and Home Screen, in the Dynamic Island and StandBy on iPhone, and in the menu bar of a paired Mac. (`live-activities`)
- **Support all four presentations: compact, minimal, expanded, and Lock Screen.** The system picks one or a combination for each device and location, and builds default appearances for other contexts from them. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) Expect the compact presentation in the Dynamic Island when only one Live Activity is active.** It has a leading element and a trailing element on either side of the TrueDepth camera, and shows up-to-date information despite its limited space. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) Expect the minimal presentation when several Live Activities are active.** The Dynamic Island shows two, one attached and one detached that appears circular or oval depending on its content size, and people tap either one to open its app. (`live-activities`)
- **Give the Lock Screen presentation, a banner at the bottom of the Lock Screen, a layout similar to the expanded presentation.** On devices without the Dynamic Island, an alert briefly shows it as a banner over the Home Screen or other apps. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) Expect StandBy to show your Live Activity in the minimal presentation, then the Lock Screen presentation scaled up 2x to fill the screen when tapped.** A custom Lock Screen background color extends across the whole screen, so the design fills it edge to edge. (`live-activities`)
- **(macOS) Expect active Live Activities in the menu bar of a paired Mac, in the compact, minimal, and expanded presentations.** Clicking one launches iPhone Mirroring to display your app, and the Mac uses the iOS dimensions. (`live-activities`)
- **Focus on the important information people need at a glance, not everything.** Prioritize what people find most useful and share it concisely. When people want more, they tap the Live Activity to open your app, where you can provide additional detail. (`live-activities`)
- **Don't use a Live Activity to display ads or promotions.** It exists to keep people informed about ongoing events and tasks, so show only information related to them. (`live-activities`)
- **Avoid displaying sensitive information.** Casual observers can see a Live Activity on the Lock Screen or Always-On display, so show an innocuous summary that people tap to see details in your app, or redact sensitive views and let people choose whether to show them. (`live-activities`)
- **Match your app's visual aesthetic and personality in both dark and light appearances.** A Live Activity that looks like your app is easier for people to recognize and creates a visual connection to it. (`live-activities`)
- **If you include a logo mark, show it without a container, and don't use the entire app icon.** A bare logo mark integrates better with the Live Activity layout. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) Don't add elements to your app that draw attention to the Dynamic Island.** Your Live Activity appears there while your app isn't in use, and other items can appear there while your app is open. (`live-activities`)
- **Use large text in a medium or heavier weight, and small text sparingly.** Large, heavier text keeps key information legible at a glance. (`live-activities`)
- **Adapt layouts and assets to every device, scale factor, and presentation.** Live Activities scale to fit various screens and their actual size may vary or change, so use the specifications as guidance and supply appropriately sized content. (`live-activities`)
- **Adjust element size and placement to use space efficiently.** Create a layout that uses only the space it needs to display its content clearly, with elements sized and placed to fit well together. (`live-activities`)
- **Use familiar layouts for custom views, starting from the templates in Apple Design Resources.** Their default system margins and recommended text sizes keep your Live Activity legible at a glance and in step with the visual language around it. (`live-activities`)
- **Use even, matching margins and concentric placement.** Even margins between rounded shapes and edges keep elements from poking into the rounded corners and creating visual tension. Give a rounded rectangle near a corner the outer corner radius minus the margin, applied with a SwiftUI container. (`live-activities`)
- **Keep content snug within a margin concentric to the outer edge, close to it without poking into the rounded shape.** To align nonrounded content with the rounded corners, blurring it in your drawing tool can make the best position easier to find. (`live-activities`)
- **Set off a block of content with an inset container shape or a thick line.** Never run content to the very edge of the Dynamic Island. (`live-activities`)
- **Change the height of the Lock Screen and expanded presentations to fit the information.** Use only the space the content needs, growing as more arrives: a rideshare app stays compact while locating a driver, then extends to show pickup time and driver details. (`live-activities`)
- **Carefully consider a custom background color and opacity, which only the Lock Screen presentation supports.** Compact, minimal, and expanded backgrounds can't be customized. With a custom Lock Screen color or image, ensure sufficient contrast, especially for tint colors on an Always-On display with reduced luminance. (`live-activities`)
- **Use bold color for text and objects to express your app's character and identity.** Live Activities in the Dynamic Island sit on a black opaque background, and bold colors make yours recognizable at a glance, set it apart from others, and tie its elements together. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) Tint the key line around the Dynamic Island to match your content.** When the background is dark, as in Dark Mode, a key line appears to set the Dynamic Island apart from other content, so choose a color consistent with your other elements. (`live-activities`)
- **Keep Live Activity animations to two seconds or less.** Beyond extending and contracting transitions, Live Activities use system and custom animations up to that limit, and the system doesn't animate on Always-On displays with reduced luminance. (`live-activities`)
- **Use animation to reinforce information and draw attention to updates.** Besides moving elements, use the default content-replace transition or custom ones built from scale, opacity, and movement, like numeric transitions for a score or a timer that fades in and out at zero. (`live-activities`)
- **Animate layout changes, moving existing elements to their new positions.** When content needs a new layout, as when the Live Activity fills the screen in StandBy, preserve as much of the old one as possible rather than removing elements and animating them back in. (`live-activities`)
- **Try to avoid overlapping elements during transitions.** Sometimes it's best to animate an element out and back in at its new position to avoid collisions. In a list, animate only the item that moves and fade the other items in and out. (`live-activities`)
- **Open your app at the right location when people tap the Live Activity.** Take people directly to related details and actions instead of making them navigate to find relevant information. (`live-activities`)
- **Offer simple, direct actions essential to the Live Activity, preferably one control so people don't tap the wrong one.** Buttons and toggles take space from information, so keep them for things people activate once or pause and resume, like music playback, workouts, or recording live audio. (`live-activities`)
- **Consider a button or toggle when people could respond to an event or progress update.** A rideshare Live Activity could include a button to contact the driver while people wait for the ride to arrive. (`live-activities`)
- **Start Live Activities when people expect them, like after a food order, and let people turn them off in the related app view.** Unexpected ones can be unwanted, and without a control like a sports app's unfollow button, people may turn them all off in Settings. (`live-activities`)
- **Offer an App Shortcut that starts your Live Activity.** App Shortcuts expose functionality to the system in various contexts, so people could start your Live Activity with the Action button on iPhone, for example. (`live-activities`)
- **Update a Live Activity only when new content is available.** If the underlying content or status hasn't changed, keep the same display until it does. (`live-activities`)
- **Alert people only for essential updates that need attention.** An alert lights up the screen, plays the notification sound by default, and shows the expanded presentation in the Dynamic Island, or a banner on devices without one. Don't alert often, and don't duplicate updates with push notifications. (`live-activities`)
- **Track multiple events with a single Live Activity that rotates through them.** Separate Live Activities make people jump between them. A sports app could use one dynamic layout that cycles through scored points, substitutions, and fouls across several matches. (`live-activities`)
- **End a Live Activity immediately when its task or event ends.** The system then removes it from the Dynamic Island at once, but it can stay on the Lock Screen and in the Mac menu bar for up to four hours. (`live-activities`)
- **Consider a custom dismissal time proportional to the Live Activity's duration, usually 15 to 30 minutes.** A summary may matter only briefly: a rideshare app could keep its ended Live Activity visible for 30 minutes so people can view the summary and leave a tip. (`live-activities`)
- **Start with the iPhone design, a standard design for each presentation, then refine it for other contexts.** A Live Activity appears at different dimensions in each place, so, depending on its functionality, add custom layouts for contexts like iPhone in StandBy. (`live-activities`)
- **In the compact presentation, show the most important information: dynamic, up to date, and easy to understand.** A sports app could display the two team logos and the score. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) Make the compact leading and trailing elements read as one piece of information.** The TrueDepth camera separates them, so use consistent color and typography to connect both sides. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) Keep compact content as narrow as possible and snug against the TrueDepth camera, with no padding between.** Try not to obscure key status bar information. Balance leading and trailing views at similar sizes, using shortened units or less precise data. (`live-activities`)
- **Link both compact elements to the same screen of related details.** When people tap a compact Live Activity, open your app directly to the related details, whether they tap the leading or the trailing element. (`live-activities`)
- **Keep your Live Activity recognizable in the minimal presentation, showing updated information rather than just a logo when possible.** The Timer app's minimal presentation shows the remaining time instead of a static icon. (`live-activities`)
- **Maintain the relative placement of elements across presentations.** The expanded presentation, which people see by touching and holding a compact or minimal one, is an enlarged version of it, so make information and layout expand predictably. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) Wrap expanded content tightly around the TrueDepth camera.** Arrange content close to the camera and avoid leaving too much room around it, to use space efficiently and diminish the camera's presence. (`live-activities`)
- **Don't replicate notification layouts in the Lock Screen presentation.** Create a unique layout specific to the information your Live Activity shows. (`live-activities`)
- **Choose colors that work on a personalized Lock Screen, using custom background or tint colors and opacity sparingly.** People customize the Lock Screen with wallpapers, tint colors, and widgets, and restraint lets your Live Activity fit their aesthetic while staying legible. (`live-activities`)
- **Ensure colors and assets keep enough contrast in Dark Mode and on an Always-On display.** The default Lock Screen background follows the light or dark appearance. Pick a custom color that suits both, or one per appearance, and verify it on an Always-On display, where the system adapts colors. (`live-activities`)
- **Verify the dismiss button's generated color, and adjust it if needed.** The system generates a matching dismiss button from your Live Activity's background and foreground colors, so check that the result matches your design. (`live-activities`)
- **Use the standard 14 pt margin on the Lock Screen to align your design with notifications.** Tighter margins can suit elements like graphics or buttons, but avoid crowding the edges and creating a cluttered appearance. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) Update your layout for StandBy.** Make sure assets look great at the larger scale, and consider a custom layout that uses the extra space. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) Consider the default background color in StandBy.** It blends your Live Activity with the device bezel for a softer look that fits people's surroundings, and lets the system scale it slightly larger because it needn't allow for margins around the TrueDepth camera. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) In StandBy, keep standard margins and keep graphics off the screen edge.** As the Live Activity grows, content without margins gets clipped and looks broken. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) Verify your StandBy design in Night Mode.** The system applies a red tint to your Live Activity, so check that its colors still provide enough contrast. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) Fit each compact element within 62.33x36.67 pt on a 430x932 pt iPhone and 52.33x36.67 pt on a 393x852 pt iPhone.** The minimal presentation is 36.67 to 45 pt wide and 36.67 pt tall on both. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) Design the expanded and Lock Screen presentations at 408 pt wide on a 430x932 pt iPhone and 371 pt wide on a 393x852 pt iPhone.** Their height ranges from 84 to 160 pt. (`live-activities`)
- **(iOS) Expect a 44 pt corner radius on the Dynamic Island, matching the TrueDepth camera's rounded shape.** In compact and minimal presentations it's 250 pt wide on Pro Max, Plus, and Air models and 230 pt on others, and expanded, 408 and 371 pt respectively. (`live-activities`)
- **(iPadOS) Design the Lock Screen presentation at 500 pt wide on a 1366x1024 pt iPad and 425 pt wide on the other listed sizes, from 1024x768 to 1194x834 pt.** Its height ranges from 84 to 160 pt. (`live-activities`)

## Notifications (`notifications`, `managing-notifications`)

- **Get permission before sending any notification, and provide an in-app settings screen where people can change their choice.** The system's settings already let people change it or silence all notifications, but you must also offer the in-app screen for informational and marketing notifications. (`managing-notifications`)
- **A notification can appear as a banner or view, a badge on your app icon, or an item in Notification Center.** Banners and views show on the Lock Screen and Home Screen, or on the Mac desktop, and people choose the styles they want in settings. (`notifications`)
- **Use communication notifications for direct communication like calls and messages, and noncommunication notifications for everything else.** Communication ones feature contact images and group names instead of your app icon and show the sender's name as the title, and the system times their alerts by the sender. (`notifications`, `managing-notifications`)
- **Send concise, informative notifications, and never several for the same thing, even if people haven't responded.** People turn them on for quick updates and read them at their convenience, so repeats fill Notification Center and may make people turn off all your notifications. (`notifications`)
- **Don't use a notification to tell people to do a task in your app, or to report an error.** Instructions are hard to remember after people dismiss the notification, so offer a notification action for simple tasks, and put errors in an alert, since the wrong component confuses people. (`notifications`)
- **Handle notifications gracefully when your app is in the foreground.** They don't appear then, so make the information discoverable but not distracting: increment a badge or subtly insert it into the current view, as Mail just lists a new message as unread in the mailbox people are viewing. (`notifications`)
- **Keep sensitive information out of notifications, and write a generic body for hidden previews.** You can't predict who will see one. With previews hidden, the system shows only your app icon and the title Notification, so sentence-case text like "Friend request" or "Shipment" gives context without details. (`notifications`)
- **People can delay your alerts with a Focus or a scheduled summary.** A Focus filters alerts during activities like sleep or work, except from the contacts and apps people let through, yet the notification itself is available as soon as it arrives. (`managing-notifications`)
- **Build trust by giving each noncommunication notification a realistic interruption level.** The system uses the level to decide when to deliver the alert, and high urgency for low-priority information can lead people to turn off all your notifications. (`managing-notifications`)
- **Choose from four interruption levels: Passive, Active (the default), Time Sensitive, and Critical.** Passive suits a restaurant recommendation people can view at leisure, Active a score update, Time Sensitive an account security issue or package delivery, and Critical urgent health and safety information. (`managing-notifications`)
- **Time Sensitive alerts override scheduled delivery and break through a Focus, and Critical alerts also override the Ring/Silent switch on iPhone and iPad.** Passive and Active override nothing. Critical needs an entitlement and is extremely rare, typically from government and public agencies or health and home apps. (`managing-notifications`)
- **Use Time Sensitive only for events happening now or within an hour.** That helps people see the benefit of letting it break through a Focus or scheduled delivery. The system explains your first one, offers a way to turn them off, and periodically asks people to reevaluate. (`managing-notifications`)
- **Send marketing, like a subscription offer, only after people explicitly opt in, and never as Time Sensitive.** Even with permission, it must never break through a Focus or scheduled delivery. Ask in an alert or modal view that describes what you'll send, with a clear opt-in and opt-out. (`managing-notifications`)
- **Add a short title only when it gives context, like a headline, event name, or email subject.** It sits at the top, where it's most visible, and without one the system shows your app name, which beats a generic title like New Document. Use title case and no ending punctuation. (`notifications`)
- **Write notification text in succinct, complete sentences, with sentence case and proper punctuation.** Don't truncate it yourself, because the system does that automatically when necessary. (`notifications`)
- **Leave your app name and icon out of a notification.** The system already shows a large version of your app icon at its leading edge, and in a communication notification, the sender's contact image badged with a small version of your icon. (`notifications`)
- **Consider a sound to distinguish your notifications, but never rely on it for important information.** It gets attention when people aren't looking, yet they may not hear it. Use a system alert sound or a short, distinctive, professionally produced custom one. You can't add vibration programmatically. (`notifications`)
- **Offer actions for common, time-saving tasks people can finish without opening your app, never one that merely opens it.** The detail view holds up to four buttons, like Calendar's Snooze, and tapping the notification already opens related content, so a duplicate button clutters the view and confuses people. (`notifications`)
- **Label each action button with a short, title-case phrase that clearly describes its result.** Leave out your app name and anything extraneous, and keep it brief so it doesn't truncate, including after localization. (`notifications`)
- **Give each notification action a simple, recognizable interface icon.** It reinforces the action's meaning so people understand it instantly. The system shows it on the trailing side of the title, and SF Symbols offers existing symbols you can use or edit. (`notifications`)
- **Prefer nondestructive actions, and if you must offer a destructive one, give people enough context to avoid unintended consequences.** The system gives actions you identify as destructive a distinct appearance. (`notifications`)
- **Use a badge only to show how many unread notifications people have, never for weather, dates and times, stock prices, or game scores.** A badge is a small filled oval with a number on your app icon, and it disappears once people address those notifications. (`notifications`)
- **Keep your badge up to date, updating it as soon as people open the corresponding notifications.** Otherwise people think new ones are waiting when they've seen them all. Reducing the count to zero also removes all related notifications from Notification Center. (`notifications`)
- **Never rely on a badge alone for essential information, and don't mimic one with a custom image or component.** People can turn badges off, so they'd miss the message, and a look-alike frustrates those who turned them off. Surface important information as soon as your app opens. (`notifications`)
