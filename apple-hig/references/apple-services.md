# Apple services and frameworks

How an app builds on In-App Purchase, Apple Pay, Wallet, iCloud, and Maps. Apple's position: use the system's own flows and UI instead of copying them, ask for payment or data only when the moment calls for it, and use Apple's names, marks, and artwork exactly as provided. (`apple-in-app-purchase`, `apple-pay`, `wallet`)

## Apple In-App Purchase (`apple-in-app-purchase`)

- **Use In-App Purchase for digital goods, and let people experience the app before asking them to buy.** Apple Pay handles physical goods, services, and donations. For subscriptions, consider a freemium app, metered paywall, or free trial. (`apple-in-app-purchase`)
- **Show the total billing price for every purchase, and make each sign-up screen state the subscription's name, duration, and what each period provides.** Offer sign-in and purchase restore, and say plainly a free trial ends in automatic payment. (`apple-in-app-purchase`)
- **Offer a range of clearly distinguishable subscription options, each with a short, self-explanatory name, a price, and a duration.** For an introductory offer, list its price, how long it lasts, and the standard price after. (`apple-in-app-purchase`)
- **Show subscription benefits during onboarding, and ask only for necessary information at sign-up.** Pair them with a strong call to action and clear terms, and defer the rest, since long sign-ups may lower conversion. (`apple-in-app-purchase`)
- **Prompt people to subscribe at relevant moments, like nearing the monthly limit of free content, and include a sign-up option in settings.** People commonly look for it there. (`apple-in-app-purchase`)
- **Encourage a new subscription only from non-subscribers, and offer sign-in for subscriptions bought elsewhere.** Otherwise people may think their subscription lapsed, or that they must pay twice. (`apple-in-app-purchase`)
- **Mention Family Sharing where people learn about your content, in wording that suits purchasers and family members.** Put "Family" or "Shareable" in names, mention it on the sign-up screen, and greet family members with "Your family subscription includes...". (`apple-in-app-purchase`)
- **Never modify or replicate the system confirmation sheet, and show your store only when people can pay.** The sheet prevents accidental purchases. If parental restrictions block payment, hide the store or explain why. (`apple-in-app-purchase`)
- **Summarize people's subscriptions with the renewal date near the management option, and consider the system's management UI.** It lets people manage or cancel without leaving your app. (`apple-in-app-purchase`)
- **Always make canceling a subscription easy.** A buried or unclear cancel action makes subscribers feel prevented from canceling. When they try, you may remind them what they'd lose, suggest another plan, or offer a discount. (`apple-in-app-purchase`)
- **Offer a purchase-help screen before people request a refund, showing each recent purchase's image, name, description, and date.** It can solve missing purchases and answer questions, beside a link to the system refund flow. (`apple-in-app-purchase`)
- **Title the refund action simply, like "Request a Refund", and keep it visible without scrolling or another screen.** The system flow already says Apple handles the refund, and help content must never become a barrier. (`apple-in-app-purchase`)
- **Offer alternatives, like immediate fulfillment or a conciliatory item, but never characterize Apple's refund policies.** Make clear people can still request a refund, and never speculate whether they'll get it. (`apple-in-app-purchase`)
- **Build custom offer codes only from alphanumeric ASCII characters, and tell people how to redeem them.** No Chinese, Arabic, or other special characters, and people redeem custom codes only through a redemption URL or your app, not App Store settings. (`apple-in-app-purchase`)
- **Support redemption in your app with a Redeem Code button, and let people enjoy unlocked content right away.** Put it in the paywall, onboarding, or settings, and welcome subscribers, even those who subscribed before first opening the app. (`apple-in-app-purchase`)

## Apple Pay (`apple-pay`)

- **Offer Apple Pay wherever it's supported, and make it the primary option when people have a card in Wallet.** If you check for an active card, Apple Pay must lead, though not alone, in the same flow, perhaps preselected. (`apple-pay`)
- **If Apple Pay is available, assume people want it: make its button prominent and never hide it.** Show it first or larger, visible without scrolling, and never smaller than other payment buttons or its minimum size and margins. Explain blockers, like a missing size, after the tap. (`apple-pay`)
- **Use only the system-provided Apple Pay buttons, and only to start payment or Apple Pay setup.** They get approved styles, localization, and VoiceOver text. A custom payment button can't say "Apple Pay" or show its logo, so show the mark or name Apple Pay on that page. (`apple-pay`)
- **Use the Apple Pay mark only to show that you accept Apple Pay, unaltered except in height, with clear space of 1/10 its height.** It's never a button and never shares its border with another graphic. (`apple-pay`)
- **In a side-by-side layout, put the Apple Pay button to the right of Add to Cart, and match its corner radius to other buttons.** It can be rounded, square, or capsule-shaped. (`apple-pay`)
- **Add Apple Pay buttons to product detail pages, and offer express checkout for the cart.** A detail-page purchase covers only that item: if it's in the cart, remove it once bought. Express checkout opens the sheet at once, with one shipping method and destination. (`apple-pay`)
- **Collect everything the sheet can't take before showing it: required options like size, optional details like gift messages, and per-item shipping methods or destinations.** The sheet has no field for optional data and allows one shipping method and destination per order. (`apple-pay`)
- **Keep the payment sheet to essentials, with short line items and "Pay [Business Name]" on the total line.** Use line items for charges, discounts, pending costs, or recurring payments, never a product list. An intermediary names both: "Pay [End Merchant] (via [Your Business])". (`apple-pay`)
- **Let people enter a coupon or promotional code in the sheet, and show any code already applied.** It spares a separate step, especially in express checkout, and reassures people. (`apple-pay`)
- **Let people choose the shipping method in the sheet, and an in-store pickup location before it.** Show each method's description, cost, and, optionally, an estimated date, let people pick a pickup window, and show the pickup address on the sheet. (`apple-pay`)
- **When the final cost is unknown at checkout, like a ride or a later tip, explain it in the sheet with an Amount Pending subtotal.** Do so where regulations allow, and show any preauthorized amount accurately. (`apple-pay`)
- **Validate data leniently, and when it's truly invalid, name the field and what's expected.** Ignore a Zip+4 code's extra digits and accept any phone format. Write "Zip code doesn't match city", not "Address is invalid": a sentence-case noun phrase, no ending punctuation, 128 characters at most. (`apple-pay`)
- **Report problems to the system with a custom message and the right status code, and cancel any in-progress payment on interruption.** The sheet then shows the most relevant error, and people restart from the button. (`apple-pay`)
- **Let the payment sheet show progress and errors, then thank people on a confirmation page.** Extra spinners confuse people about the transaction's state. Don't require an account before purchase: invite registration on the confirmation page, prefilled from checkout. (`apple-pay`)
- **Before a recurring payment, make sure people understand its billing frequency and terms, and state the amount billed now in the total line.** The sheet can show the billing frequency too. (`apple-pay`)
- **Use line items to restate billing frequency, discounts, upfront fees, and trial terms.** For a trial, show its amount ($0 if free), the regular amount after, and when regular billing begins. If nothing is charged now, say when billing starts. (`apple-pay`)
- **Show the payment sheet for a subscription change only when it adds fees.** A change that costs the same or less needs no authorization. (`apple-pay`)
- **For donations, offer preset amounts plus Other Amount, and identify the donation in a line item.** Presets like $25, $50, and $100 cut steps, and "Donation $50.00" reminds people what they're authorizing. (`apple-pay`)
- **Write "Apple Pay" as two words with capital A and P, and describe it in text only when every payment option is text-only.** Otherwise use the Apple Pay mark. All caps fits only an established all-caps style. (`apple-pay`)

## Wallet (`wallet`)

- **Offer to add a pass when an action creates it, or at next launch if made elsewhere, and never ask twice.** System UI adds it in one tap, and Apple Pay purchases can add their orders. Keep an Add to Apple Wallet button where the pass appears. (`wallet`)
- **Add related passes as a group, and let people jump from your app to a pass in Wallet.** Add a multi-connection flight's boarding passes at once, bundle event tickets on your website, and label the link like "View in Wallet". (`wallet`)
- **Supply relevance and expiration data, and get permission before deleting a pass.** A gym card then appears on the Lock Screen as people arrive, and expired passes hide. Let people choose manual or automatic removal, or confirm with an alert. (`wallet`)
- **Keep passes up to date, but interrupt only for time-critical changes.** A boarding pass can show delays and gate changes live: a gate change warrants a change message, a new support number doesn't, and marketing never does. (`wallet`)
- **Design a clean pass that feels at home in Wallet rather than copying the physical card, with essentials in the header.** The header stays visible when passes are collapsed, so put the event date or account balance there. (`wallet`)
- **Make a pass instantly identifiable with brand colors and imagery, and keep its text legible.** Use images, icons, and full-art backgrounds, with label colors that contrast with solid backgrounds and images alike. (`wallet`)
- **Use pass images only for visual content, and keep their files small.** Embedded text isn't accessible, so use text fields and semantic tags, and add barcodes with Pass Designer or the APIs. Small files download fast. (`wallet`)
- **Provide a pass icon, and avoid inner drop shadows on logo artwork.** The icon represents the pass on the Lock Screen, in Mail, and in Wallet, and can be your app icon. Inner shadows hurt the logo's legibility. (`wallet`)
- **Make order information available as soon as people order, and report fulfillment status as it changes.** Start with what you have, like "Check back later for full order details". Status updates, from Order Placed to Delivered, Issue, or Canceled, can notify customers. (`wallet`)
- **Supply 300x300-pixel PNG or JPEG logo and product images on nontransparent backgrounds.** They appear at several sizes, so show products plainly, not in a lifestyle scene or on a busy background. (`wallet`)
- **Describe each item so people can verify the order, and link to where they manage it.** Give line items a price, name, and image, and use a universal link, which opens even without your app installed. (`wallet`)
- **Match shipping statuses to what you know, and describe an Issue or Canceled status directly and thoroughly.** Name the carrier or keep "Track Shipment", use step statuses only with carrier data, and link to tracking. Say what went wrong and what people can do. (`wallet`)
- **Offer several ways to contact the merchant, at minimum a link to its website.** Messages for Business, phone, email, and a support page can join it in the Contact menu. (`wallet`)
- **Request identity verification only when a transaction needs it, and only the data it needs.** Unrelated requests make people suspicious, so don't ask during account creation. Label the button Verify Age, Verify Identity, Continue, or Verify with Apple Wallet by use case. (`wallet`)
- **Show Verify with Apple Wallet only when the device can return the requested data, and offer a fallback method otherwise.** (`wallet`)
- **Explain in your purpose string why you need identity data, and say whether and how long you'll keep it.** The verification sheet shows the string, and setting a retention period adds an explanation automatically. (`wallet`)

## iCloud (`icloud`)

- **Make iCloud work automatically, and never ask which documents to keep there.** People turn it on in Settings and expect all their content there. If a choice is needed, offer it once at first launch: all data in iCloud, or none. (`icloud`)
- **Store only what people create and understand, and sync app state across devices.** iCloud is finite and people pay for it, so leave out app resources and regenerable content. A magazine app can store the last page viewed to resume on another device. (`icloud`)
- **Keep content up to date when possible, within storage and bandwidth limits.** For very large documents, let people choose when to download updates, show that a newer version exists, and give subtle feedback if a download takes more than a few seconds. (`icloud`)
- **Handle sync problems quietly: no alert when iCloud is unavailable, and early, unobtrusive conflict resolution.** A brief note that changes won't sync until access returns is enough. Resolve conflicts automatically if possible, else with a notification that tells versions apart. (`icloud`)
- **Warn and ask for confirmation before deleting a document, and include iCloud content in search.** Deleting removes it from iCloud and every other device, and people expect their content everywhere, search included. (`icloud`)
- **For games, consider saving player progress in iCloud with GameSave.** It syncs saves across devices, with built-in alerts for offline syncing issues and conflicts. (`icloud`)

## Maps (`maps`)

- **Make your map interactive, and never cover the Apple logo and legal link permanently.** People expect familiar zooming and panning. Keep the logo and link fixed to the map, padded 7 pt at the sides and 10 pt above and below. (`maps`)
- **Help people find places with search and category filters, and clearly mark what they select.** A mall map might filter by clothing or toys, and an outline with color variation marks a selected area. (`maps`)
- **Style annotations and indoor maps to match your app, not Apple Maps, and cluster overlapping points.** Replace the default red marker with your colors, an image, or two to three characters. A cluster expands into individual points as people zoom in. (`maps`)
- **Choose the default emphasis style for standard maps, and the muted one when your content must stand out.** The default stays aligned with Apple Maps when people switch. Give custom controls contrast with a thin stroke or light shadow. (`maps`)
- **Pick a place card style that fits your map without duplicating what you show.** A small map with many annotations suits the compact callout, and a caption complements information you already display. Outside a map view, a place card must include a map. (`maps`)
- **Keep place cards readable at every device and window size, and cue that people can open one.** Give a full callout a minimum width, and pair place names and addresses with a details button, or a name with a map pin icon. (`maps`)
- **In an indoor map, add detail as people zoom in, and style each kind of feature distinctly.** An airport shows terminals and gates zoomed out, stores and restrooms zoomed in. Color with icons sets apart areas, stores, and services. (`maps`)
- **Offer a floor picker for venues with several levels, and limit scrolling outside the venue.** Concise floor numbers usually suffice. Keep part of the indoor map onscreen so a hard swipe doesn't get people lost. (`maps`)
- **Show the surrounding area for context, and support navigation to nearby transit.** Dim noninteractive streets and playgrounds in a distinct color, and route to bus stops, train stations, and parking, or switch to Apple Maps. (`maps`)
