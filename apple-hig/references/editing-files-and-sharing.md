# Editing, files, and sharing

How people change content, move it, keep it, and send it elsewhere. Apple says to build on the system edit menu, undo, drag and drop, file browsers, and share sheet people already know, make every change predictable and reversible instead of asking for confirmation, save work automatically, and offer only the actions that apply in the current context. (`edit-menus`, `undo-and-redo`, `drag-and-drop`, `file-management`, `activity-views`)

## Undo and redo (`undo-and-redo`)

- **Help people predict what undo and redo will do.** People often undo repeatedly and forget which action an undo targets, so name the result in menu items like Undo Typing or Redo Bold, and on iPhone describe it in the shake-to-undo alert. (`undo-and-redo`)
- **Show the result of every undo and redo, even offscreen.** Otherwise people think nothing happened and repeat the action, so undoing the deletion of a paragraph that's out of view should scroll to the restored paragraph. (`undo-and-redo`)
- **Let people undo many times, without unnecessary limits.** People expect to undo every action they've performed since a logical step like opening a document or saving their work. (`undo-and-redo`)
- **Consider letting people revert several changes at once.** A batch of related actions, like incremental adjustments to one property, can undo in one step, and people may want to undo everything since they opened or saved the document. (`undo-and-redo`)
- **Offer undo through the system's menus, shortcuts, and gestures, adding undo and redo buttons only when necessary.** People expect the macOS Edit menu, keyboard shortcuts on Mac or iPad, or shaking iPhone. Dedicated buttons use the standard symbols in a toolbar. (`undo-and-redo`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Never redefine the standard undo gestures, a three-finger swipe or shaking iPhone.** Redefining standard gestures confuses people and makes the experience unpredictable. (`undo-and-redo`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Describe the operation in a word or two after the undo alert's automatic prefix.** The title already begins with "Undo " or "Redo ", so finish it briefly and precisely, like Undo Name or Redo Address Change. (`undo-and-redo`)
- **(macOS) Put Undo and Redo at the top of the Edit menu, with Command-Z and Shift-Command-Z.** Mac users expect to find them there and to use these standard shortcuts. (`undo-and-redo`)

## Edit menus (`edit-menus`)

- **Use the system edit menu, revealed by the interactions people already know.** A custom menu with the same commands is redundant and confusing, and people expect to touch and hold on a touchscreen or secondary click with a trackpad or keyboard, not learn a custom interaction. (`edit-menus`)
- **Expect edit commands to apply to any selectable content, text or not.** Images, files, contact cards, charts, and map locations can all take them, and in iOS and iPadOS the system can add an action for detected data, like Get Directions for an address. (`edit-menus`)
- **(iOS) Expect a compact, horizontal edit menu when people touch and hold or double-tap to select content.** A chevron on its trailing edge expands it into a context menu. (`edit-menus`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Make the edit menu work in both its compact horizontal and vertical styles.** Multi-Touch gestures reveal the compact style, while a keyboard or pointing device reveals the vertical one, which iPadOS opens directly as a context menu. (`edit-menus`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Move the edit menu if it would cover important content or parts of the interface.** By default it appears above or below the insertion point or selection, with a pointer to the target. You can change its position, but not its shape or pointer. (`edit-menus`)
- **(macOS) Offer editing commands in a context menu people reveal while editing, and in the menu bar's Edit menu.** These are the two places where people access editing commands on a Mac. (`edit-menus`)
- **Show only commands that apply to the current context, removing or dimming the rest.** With nothing selected, don't offer Copy or Cut, and with nothing to paste, don't offer Paste. (`edit-menus`)
- **Place a few custom commands next to related system ones, labeled with a verb or short verb phrase.** Custom formatting commands follow the system ones in the format section, preserving the order people expect, and too many custom commands overwhelm. (`edit-menus`)
- **Support undo and redo, because edit menu commands act without asking for confirmation.** Like all menus, the edit menu acts immediately, so undo and redo are how people return to a previous state. (`edit-menus`)
- **Avoid other controls that duplicate edit menu commands.** People expect familiar edit commands in the edit menu or as standard keyboard shortcuts, and redundant controls take space from actions people don't already know. (`edit-menus`)
- **Let people select and copy noneditable content text, but not control labels.** People like pasting static content, such as an image caption or a social media status, into a message, note, or web search. (`edit-menus`)
- **Distinguish deletion commands when necessary.** Delete behaves like pressing the Delete key, while Cut copies the selection to the pasteboard before deleting it. (`edit-menus`)

## Drag and drop (`drag-and-drop`)

- **Support drag and drop throughout the app.** Most people know it and try it everywhere, and system components like text fields and text views support it without extra work. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Always offer another way to do what drag and drop does.** Dragging is sometimes inconvenient or impossible, so add menu commands to copy and move items, and in iOS and iPadOS identify sources and destinations to assistive technologies. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Expect people to drag with touch, a pointer, full keyboard access, or VoiceOver.** iOS and iPadOS support gestures, pointing devices, and full keyboard access, a Mac adds VoiceOver, and Universal Control lets people drag content between a Mac and an iPad. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Move content within a container, copy it between containers, and always copy between apps.** Moved content exists only in the destination, copied content in both. Before changing these defaults, prefer what's least likely to cause frustration or data loss. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **With a physical keyboard attached, check for the Option key at drop time.** Holding Option makes a drag within the same container copy, and releasing it before the drop makes it a move again. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Support dragging several items at once when it makes sense.** People prefer dragging a group to dragging items one by one. iOS, iPadOS, and macOS let people drag a multiple selection, and macOS also accepts items selected from several apps. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Let people perform multiple simultaneous drag activities.** In iPadOS people can gather more Home Screen app icons into a drag in progress, so let people add items during a drag, showing them flocking, and accept simultaneous drops. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Let people undo a drop, or confirm first when it can't be undone.** The Finder confirms before a file goes into a write-only folder people couldn't open to fix a mistake, and Photos lets people cancel sharing after a drop into a shared photo stream. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Offer dragged content in several representations, highest fidelity first.** The destination then takes the best version it accepts: a drawing can come as PDF, then lossless PNG, then lossy JPEG, and a chart as a native object, then an image. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **As a destination, take the richest version you can accept, and extract only the relevant part.** Show a native chart object if your app supports charts, and its image if not. A contact dropped in Mail's recipient field shows only the name and email address. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Consider supporting spring loading.** Dragging content over a button or segmented control can activate it, as Calendar moves an event over the toolbar's day, week, month, or year segments. On a Mac it takes a Magic Trackpad force click, and on iPad a hover. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Show a translucent drag image once the selection moves about 3 points, and keep it until the drop.** Translucency distinguishes it from the original content and lets people see destinations as they pass over them. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Change the drag image only when it helps people predict the result.** A photo dragged into a document can grow to its default size there, and flocking groups several items so people can check none is missing. Never make it change constantly or radically. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Highlight a destination only while content is over it, and only if it can accept the drop.** Otherwise show nothing or a not-allowed image, like the circle.slash symbol, and with several possible destinations, help people identify one at a time. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Give visual feedback when a drop fails or lands on an invalid destination.** The item can move back to its source, if it's still visible, or scale up and fade out as if evaporating. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Scroll a destination's content when people drag inside a long scrolling container.** It helps people find the right place to drop, stops once they drag outside the container, and comes by default in system text views and text fields. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Show feedback when dropped content needs time to transfer.** A progress indicator helps people estimate the wait, and in collections, lists, and tables, a placeholder shows where the content will appear. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Provide feedback when a drop starts a task or action.** When people drop content on a control that starts a task, such as printing, show that the task has begun and keep people informed of its progress. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Keep dropped text's styling when the destination supports it, and otherwise apply the destination's style.** Shared text styles preserve the original font, typeface, size, and other attributes. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **Keep dropped content selected in the destination, and update the source.** People expect to act on it right away. A copy within one container deselects the original, and a drag to another container deselects the source. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **(macOS) Consider letting people drag content into the Finder in a format your app can reopen.** Calendar turns a dragged event into an .ics file people can share or drag back, and dragged text becomes a clipping, unrelated to the Clipboard, that people can later drop into a text field. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **(macOS) Let people drag a background selection without activating its window.** People expect to drag selected content from an inactive window to the active one without bringing the inactive window forward. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **(macOS) Let people drag individual items from an inactive window without changing its background selection.** People can drag an unselected file from an inactive Finder window without deselecting the window's selected files. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **(macOS) Consider a badge showing how many items people are dragging.** It's a small filled oval with a number, updated when a destination accepts only some of the items. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **(macOS) Consider changing the pointer to show what a drop will do.** Besides the copy pointer, use the drag link, disappearing item, or operation not allowed pointer as the situation requires. (`drag-and-drop`)
- **(macOS) Let people select and drag content in one motion.** Unless people are selecting multiple items, they appreciate not having to pause between selecting and starting the drag. (`drag-and-drop`)

## Collaboration and sharing (`collaboration-and-sharing`)

- **Build sharing and collaboration on the system share sheet and Messages, whatever your backend.** People start a collaboration by dropping a document into a Messages conversation or picking a destination in the share sheet, with CloudKit, iCloud Drive, or a custom solution that supports universal links. (`collaboration-and-sharing`)
- **Place the Share button in a convenient location, like a toolbar.** It makes starting to share or collaborate easy, and the system share sheet in iOS and sharing popover in iPadOS and macOS let people choose a sharing method and set permissions. (`collaboration-and-sharing`)
- **If necessary, customize the share sheet or sharing popover for the kinds of file sharing your app supports.** For "send copy," pass the file with your CloudKit collaboration object, rely on iCloud Drive's default support, or include the file or a plain-text version in a custom collaboration object. (`collaboration-and-sharing`)
- **Summarize your sharing permissions in a short phrase, like "Only invited people can edit" or "Everyone can make changes."** The system puts the summary in a button that reveals the options people use to define the collaboration. (`collaboration-and-sharing`)
- **Keep the sharing options behind that button few, simple, and grouped.** Offer choices like who can access the content, whether they can edit or only read, and whether collaborators can add participants, grouped so people understand them at a glance. (`collaboration-and-sharing`)
- **Show the Collaboration button prominently, next to the Share button, as soon as collaboration starts.** It reminds people the content is shared and identifies who's sharing it. (`collaboration-and-sharing`)
- **Add only essential custom actions to the collaboration popover, and only if needed.** It lists collaborators and Messages and FaceTime buttons on top, your items in the middle, and a button to manage the shared file at the bottom. Notes adds just a summary of recent updates. (`collaboration-and-sharing`)
- **Retitle the Manage Shared File button if another name fits your app better.** It opens the view where people change settings and add or remove collaborators, which CloudKit sharing provides and other solutions must build. (`collaboration-and-sharing`)
- **Consider posting collaboration events as Messages notifications, each with a universal link.** Changes to the content or membership and mentions of a participant can each open the relevant view in your app. (`collaboration-and-sharing`)

## Activity views and extensions (`activity-views`)

- **(iOS, iPadOS) Open the activity view, or share sheet, only from the Share button.** People expect system activities behind Share, while viewing a page or document or after selecting an item, and another route confuses them. It appears as a sheet or popover depending on device and orientation. (`activity-views`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Offer app-specific activities, which the share sheet lists before system-wide ones.** Photos adds Copy Photo, Add to Album, and Adjust Location ahead of actions like Add to Files or AirPlay, and people can edit the list to show what they use most. (`activity-views`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Don't duplicate system activities, and give similar app-specific ones a distinct title.** People can't tell a duplicate Print action from the system one, so a bank app's custom print format becomes Print Transaction. (`activity-views`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Keep activities appropriate to the current context.** You can't reorder system tasks, but you can exclude ones that don't apply, like Print in an app with nothing to print, and choose which custom tasks appear. (`activity-views`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Title a custom action with a single verb or brief verb phrase, without your company or product name.** Long titles wrap and may truncate. A share activity differs: the share sheet shows its title, typically a company name, below its icon. (`activity-views`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Represent a custom activity with an SF Symbol, or center a custom interface icon in about 70x70 pixels.** SF Symbols offers a comprehensive set of configurable symbols for items and concepts in an activity view. (`activity-views`)
- **Use a share extension to send content to apps and services, and an action extension for content-specific tasks.** Action extensions handle tasks like adding a bookmark or editing an inline image without leaving the current context. iOS and iPadOS show both in the share sheet, and both work on a Mac. (`activity-views`)
- **(macOS) Expect people to open a share extension from a toolbar Share button or a context menu's Share item.** Action extensions open from embedded content under the pointer, like an image in a Mail compose window, a toolbar button, or a Finder quick action. (`activity-views`)
- **Make an extension's interface feel familiar and tied to your app.** Prefer the system composition view for a share extension, include your app name in an action extension, and show elements of your app's interface if you present one. (`activity-views`)
- **Streamline and limit an extension's interaction to a few steps.** A share extension can post an image to a social media account with a single tap or click. (`activity-views`)
- **Never put a modal view above your extension, beyond a necessary alert.** The system already presents the extension in a modal view. (`activity-views`)
- **Give an action extension a symbol or interface icon that identifies its task.** A share extension automatically uses your app icon, which gives people confidence that your app provided it. (`activity-views`)
- **Report a lengthy extension task's progress in your main app, and notify people only about a problem.** The activity view dismisses as soon as people finish, so continue long work in the background, and don't notify people simply because it completes. (`activity-views`)

## File management (`file-management`)

- **Save work automatically, so it's preserved unless people cancel or delete it.** Don't make saving an explicit step: save periodically while people edit, and when they close a file or switch apps. (`file-management`)
- **Offer New and Open through familiar menus and keyboard shortcuts, plus an Add (+) button.** In macOS they live in the File menu, the add action included, and in iPadOS in the shortcuts interface people see while holding Command. (`file-management`)
- **Build any custom file browser on people's understanding of the Finder and Files.** It can open on the most relevant place, like a Documents or iCloud folder or the last location, but must let people reach the rest of the file system. (`file-management`)
- **Hide file extensions by default, but let people show them.** Reflect the current choice in every open and save interface you display. (`file-management`)
- **Use a Quick Look viewer to preview files your app can't open.** People can preview attached or unsupported files without leaving your app, and even interact, like listening to audio, marking up a photo, or rotating and scaling a 3D file. (`file-management`)
- **Consider a Quick Look generator for custom file types.** It lets other apps, including the Finder, Files, and Spotlight, preview your documents, which makes them easier to find. (`file-management`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Consider the system document launcher for browsing, opening, and creating files.** It's a full-screen view with a title card holding your app name and two buttons, a background with accessory images around the card, and a sheet with a file browser and optional controls. (`file-management`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Give the title card's two buttons your app's most important functions.** The primary button typically creates a document and the secondary offers more options: Numbers uses Start Writing and Choose a Template. (`file-management`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Make the launcher's background clearly distinct from the accessories and title card.** Use a solid color, a gradient, or a pattern, and avoid complex images or patterns that distract from the foreground elements. (`file-management`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Place accessories so the app name and both buttons stay clearly visible.** Accessories in front of and behind the title card create depth, but too many clutter it, so test across screen sizes and orientations. (`file-management`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Animate the launcher's accessories sparingly.** Too much motion confuses or disorients people, so prefer gentle, repeating animations, like an accessory that seems to breathe or sway softly. (`file-management`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) In a file provider extension, list only documents that fit the current context.** The extension lets other apps import, export, open, and move your documents, so a PDF-editing app should see only PDFs, perhaps with modification dates, sizes, and local or remote status. (`file-management`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Let people choose a destination when exporting or moving documents.** Unless you store documents in a single directory, let people navigate your hierarchy, and consider letting them add subdirectories. (`file-management`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Don't add a custom top toolbar to a file provider extension.** It loads in a modal view that already has a toolbar, so a second one confuses people and takes space from content. (`file-management`)
- **(macOS) Use the default file browser unless you have an important reason to build your own.** People have strong associations with the file browsing of the Finder and most document-based apps. (`file-management`)
- **(macOS) Make a custom open interface convenient.** Offer Open Recent beside Open, filters, or opening several documents at once, and retitle the open panel's Open button for the task, like Insert. (`file-management`)
- **(macOS) Provide a save interface for a file's name, format, and location.** A new document is Untitled until people name it, and the browser should default to a logical location. If you support several formats, let people choose one. (`file-management`)
- **(macOS) Consider adding useful options to the Save dialog in a custom accessory view.** Mail's dialog for saving messages as files offers an option to include attachments. (`file-management`)
- **(macOS) If your app syncs local and remote files, consider a Finder Sync extension.** It shows sync status and control in the Finder through item badges, contextual menu items for tasks like favoriting, and toolbar buttons for global actions like starting a sync. (`file-management`)
- **(macOS) When people turn autosave off, show unsaved changes and ask before closing.** They do it with "Ask to keep changes when closing documents" in Desktop & Dock settings, so show a save dialog when people close the document, quit, log out, or restart. (`file-management`)
- **(macOS) Mark unsaved changes with a dot only when autosave is off.** Put it on the close button and beside the document's name in the Window menu, since with autosave on it implies action is needed. An Edited title suffix works either way until the next save. (`file-management`)

## Printing (`printing`)

- **Put the print action where people expect it.** That's a Print item in the macOS File menu, or a toolbar button that opens an action sheet in iOS and iPadOS. A Print button in a macOS toolbar works best as an optional item people add by customizing. (`printing`)
- **Offer printing only when it's possible.** With nothing to print or no printers available, dim the macOS Print item, remove Print from the iOS and iPadOS action sheet, and dim or hide a custom print button. (`printing`)
- **Present only relevant options in the system print view.** Offer a page range, multiple copies, or two-sided printing only when they make sense and the printer supports them. (`printing`)
- **(macOS) Put app-specific options in a custom print panel category with a unique name, such as your app's.** Keynote adds options like printing presenter notes and skipped slides beside the default Layout, Paper Handling, and Media & Quality categories. (`printing`)
- **(macOS) Keep rarely changed page settings in a page setup dialog, without reimplementing system options.** It holds page size, orientation, and scaling for a document, and the system already handles options like printing in reverse order. (`printing`)
- **(macOS) Make dependencies between print options clear, and hide advanced ones behind a disclosure control labeled Advanced Options.** When double-sided printing is available, printing on transparencies becomes unavailable, for example. (`printing`)
- **(macOS) Let people preview a setting's effect, and keep modified settings with the document.** Update a thumbnail when a tone control changes, and at minimum keep settings until the document closes in case people print again. (`printing`)
