# Navigation and search

How people move between an app's areas and find things in it. For Apple, navigation stays visible, stable, and predictable, each component has one job (tab bars move between sections, toolbars hold actions for the current view, sidebars reach top-level areas when there's room), and search lives in one clear place with a visible scope. (`tab-bars`, `toolbars`, `sidebars`, `searching`)

**Contents:** Choosing the component · Tab bars · Sidebars · Toolbars · Page controls and path controls · Search fields · Searching · Key source articles

## Choosing the component

- **Tab bar for sections, toolbar for actions.** A tab bar moves between top-level sections, preserving each one's navigation state, and never holds actions. Controls that act on the current view belong in a toolbar: Clock's tabs are Alarm, Stopwatch, and Timer. (`tab-bars`, `toolbars`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Consider a tab bar before a sidebar.** A sidebar needs a lot of space, while a tab bar leaves more room for content and can convert to a sidebar-style appearance for less-used areas, so many apps needn't choose. (`sidebars`, `tab-bars`)
- **Past two levels of hierarchy, use a split view.** A sidebar should generally show two levels at most. Deeper hierarchies get a content list between the sidebar and the detail view. (`sidebars`)
- **Page controls are only for flat, ordered pages.** They can't express hierarchy or nonsequential relationships, so complex navigation needs a sidebar or split view. (`page-controls`)

## Tab bars (`tab-bars`)

- **Keep the tab bar visible in every section.** Hidden, it lets people forget where they are. Only a modal view may cover it, because a modal is temporary and self-contained. (`tab-bars`)
- **Use as many tabs as navigation needs, and no more.** Fewer tabs are easier to navigate, so weigh each tab's added complexity against how often people need its section. For a complex structure, consider a sidebar or a tab bar that adapts to one. (`tab-bars`)
- **Avoid overflow tabs.** When horizontal space runs out, the trailing tab becomes a More tab in iOS and iPadOS that lists the rest separately, which makes hidden content harder to reach and notice. (`tab-bars`)
- **Never disable or hide a tab, even when its content is unavailable.** Tabs that come and go make the interface look unstable and unpredictable. Explain why a section is empty instead. (`tab-bars`)
- **Label every tab, in one word when possible.** A label beneath or beside the tab's icon helps navigation by clearly describing the content or functionality the tab contains. (`tab-bars`)
- **Use SF Symbols for tab icons, preferably filled.** Symbols adapt automatically between compact layouts, icon above label, and regular ones, side by side, and filled ones match the platform. Apple Design Resources gives custom icon dimensions. (`tab-bars`)
- **Reserve badges for critical information.** A badge, a red oval with white text showing a number or an exclamation point, flags new or updated content that needs attention, and overuse dilutes its meaning. (`tab-bars`)
- **Don't color tab labels like the content layer behind them.** When the content is already bright and colorful, prefer a monochromatic tab bar, or an accent color with enough visual differentiation. (`tab-bars`)
- **(iOS) Place the tab bar floating above content at the bottom of the screen, with any search tab at the trailing end.** Its items rest on a Liquid Glass background that lets the content beneath peek through. (`tab-bars`)
- **(iOS) With an attached accessory like Music's MiniPlayer, consider minimizing the tab bar as people scroll down.** The accessory moves inline with the minimized bar, and tapping a tab or scrolling to the top restores it. (`tab-bars`)
- **(iPadOS) Prefer a tab bar for navigation, near the top of the screen, fixed or convertible to a sidebar.** It reaches the sections people use most, and a convert button gives complex apps a wider set of options. A sidebar that never converts uses a navigation split view instead. (`tab-bars`)
- **(iPadOS) Let people customize the tab bar, starting from five or fewer default tabs.** People add frequent items, like a favorite playlist in Music, or remove rare ones, and five or fewer defaults keep compact and regular sizes consistent. (`tab-bars`)

## Sidebars (`sidebars`)

- **Place a sidebar on the leading side for moving between areas or top-level collections, like folders and playlists.** It needs a lot of vertical and horizontal space, so where space is limited a more compact tab bar may navigate better. (`sidebars`)
- **Let rich content extend beneath the sidebar.** In iOS, iPadOS, and macOS the sidebar floats above content in the Liquid Glass layer, so let content scroll horizontally beneath it or use a background extension effect, which mirrors adjacent content under it. (`sidebars`)
- **Let people customize the sidebar's contents when possible.** People can then decide which areas matter most and in what order they appear. (`sidebars`)
- **Group a large hierarchy with disclosure controls.** They keep the sidebar's vertical space at a manageable level. (`sidebars`)
- **Keep group titles succinct and descriptive when the sidebar shows two levels.** Omit unnecessary words to keep the labels short. (`sidebars`)
- **Represent sidebar items with familiar SF Symbols, and make a custom symbol rather than a bitmap when you need your own.** SF Symbols offers a wide range of customizable symbols for the items in an app. (`sidebars`)
- **Let people hide the sidebar with familiar interactions, but never hide it by default.** People hide it for more room or less distraction: an edge swipe in iPadOS, a button or View > Show Sidebar and Hide Sidebar in macOS. Hidden by default, it loses discoverability. (`sidebars`)
- **Give sidebar icon colors a clear purpose.** Icons use the app's accent color by default, and in macOS people expect them to follow the system accent color they choose. Fixed colors, used sparingly, can carry meaning, like Mail's yellow VIP icon. (`sidebars`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) With the adaptable sidebar style, choose whether the app opens with a sidebar or a tab bar.** Both include a button for switching between them, and the style adapts to the platform, rotation, and window width. (`sidebars`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) If you don't build the sidebar in SwiftUI, apply the sidebar appearance of a collection view list layout.** That gives the list the correct appearance for a sidebar. (`sidebars`)
- **(macOS) Expect a sidebar's row height, text, and glyph size to follow its small, medium, or large size.** You can set the size, and people can change it by choosing a sidebar icon size in General settings. (`sidebars`)
- **(macOS) Collapse the sidebar automatically as the window narrows, and keep critical items away from its bottom.** Mail collapses its sidebar to make room for messages, and people often move windows so the bottom edge is hidden. (`sidebars`)

## Toolbars (`toolbars`)

- **Build a toolbar from three kinds of content: the view's title, navigation controls, and actions.** It arranges sets of controls horizontally along the top or bottom edge in logical sections, with back, forward, and search for navigation and buttons and menus for actions. (`toolbars`)
- **Place items in three areas: leading edge, center, and trailing edge.** Leading holds back, the sidebar toggle, the title, and an optional document menu, and isn't customizable, so they're always available. Center holds common actions, customizable in iPadOS and macOS, that collapse into the overflow menu as the window shrinks. (`toolbars`)
- **Keep the trailing edge for items that must stay available at every window size.** It holds inspector buttons, an optional search field, the More menu, and the primary action, like Done. Pin items to each area, with space between items where appropriate. (`toolbars`)
- **Choose toolbar items deliberately to avoid overcrowding, and define which ones move to the overflow menu as the toolbar narrows.** People must be able to tell each item apart and activate it. In iPadOS and macOS the system adds the overflow menu, so don't add one by hand, and avoid layouts that overflow by default. (`toolbars`)
- **Add a More menu for less important actions only if you really need it.** Try to fit every action in the toolbar first. (`toolbars`)
- **Prioritize the actions that support people's main tasks.** These are usually the commands people use most, though some apps put first the commands for the highest-level or most important objects people work with. (`toolbars`)
- **Let the content layer set the toolbar's look, not custom backgrounds or tinted controls.** Custom appearances can interfere with the system's background effects, so use a scroll edge effect when you need to separate toolbar from content. (`toolbars`)
- **Don't color toolbar item labels like the content layer behind them.** When the content is already bright and colorful, prefer the toolbar's default monochromatic appearance. (`toolbars`)
- **Prefer standard components, and keep custom ones concentric with the bar.** Standard buttons, text fields, headers, and footers have corner radii concentric with the bar's corners by default. (`toolbars`)
- **Make every item's meaning clear with simple, recognizable symbols, using text only where a symbol fails, like Edit.** People shouldn't have to guess or experiment to learn what an item does. (`toolbars`)
- **Prefer system symbols without borders.** They're familiar, get the right coloring and vibrancy, and respond consistently, and the section already provides a visible container while the system draws hover and selection states. (`toolbars`)
- **Give one key action, like Done or Submit, the prominent style on the trailing side.** It becomes a separated, tinted focal point, so specify only one primary action. (`toolbars`)
- **Group items by function and frequency, in three groups at most.** Keynote groups presentation, playback, and insertion commands, and too many groups feel cluttered and confusing even with the space on iPad and Mac. (`toolbars`)
- **Give navigation controls and critical actions like Done, Close, or Save their own familiar, visually distinct sections.** That reflects their importance and helps people discover and understand them. (`toolbars`)
- **Keep groupings and placement consistent across platforms.** People build familiarity and trust that the app behaves the same wherever they use it. (`toolbars`)
- **Keep text-labeled actions apart from symbol actions and from each other.** Side by side they read as one combined action or run together, so add fixed space between them. (`toolbars`)
- **Use the standard Back and Close buttons with their symbols, not the words.** People know Back retraces the hierarchy and Close dismisses a modal view. A custom version must look and behave the same and fit the interface, consistently throughout the app. (`toolbars`)
- **Put navigation controls in a toolbar at the top of the window, often with a search field.** It helps people move through a hierarchy of content, and in iOS this toolbar is sometimes called a navigation bar. (`toolbars`)
- **Give each window a useful title, or leave the title area empty when a title would be redundant.** A title confirms where people are and tells windows apart. Notes leaves a lone note window untitled because its first line gives context, and the system titles separate note windows with that line. (`toolbars`)
- **Write a concise title under 15 characters, and never use the app name.** A word or short phrase leaves room for other controls, and the app name says nothing about the content hierarchy or the window. (`toolbars`)
- **Hide toolbars only contextually, for a distraction-free experience, with a reliable way to bring them back.** People sometimes appreciate a minimal interface that reveals more content. (`toolbars`)
- **(iOS) Include only the most essential actions in the main toolbar area, and put others in a More menu.** Space is so limited that you need to consider carefully which actions are essential. (`toolbars`)
- **(iOS) Use a large title that shrinks on scroll and returns at the top.** It keeps people oriented as they navigate and scroll. (`toolbars`)
- **(iPadOS, macOS) Consider letting people customize the toolbar.** It helps most in apps with many items or advanced features not everyone needs, and in apps used for long periods, like editing tools that vary by work style and project. (`toolbars`)
- **(iPadOS) Consider combining a toolbar with a tab bar in the same horizontal space at the top.** It suits layouts that move between a few main areas while keeping the window's full width for content. (`toolbars`)
- **(macOS) Expect the toolbar at the top of the window frame, below or integrated with the title bar.** Window titles can sit inline with controls, and toolbar items have no bezel. (`toolbars`)
- **(macOS) Make every toolbar item also a menu bar command.** People can hide or customize the toolbar, so it can't be the only route, though not every menu command needs a toolbar item. (`toolbars`)

## Page controls and path controls (`page-controls`, `path-controls`)

- **Center a page control horizontally near the bottom of the view or window.** People then always know where to find it. (`page-controls`)
- **Show no more than about 10 dots.** More can't be counted at a glance, so past 10 peer pages use a grid or another arrangement that lets people navigate in any order. (`page-controls`)
- **Customize the default indicator image only when it adds meaning, like bookmark.fill when every page holds bookmarks.** By default the dots are equidistant, with a solid dot for the current page, and they clip when too many fit. (`page-controls`)
- **Use at most two indicator images.** One special page, like Weather's current location, can get its own image, but several unique images force people to memorize meanings and look messy. (`page-controls`)
- **Keep custom indicator images simple, and let the system color them.** Complex shapes, negative space, text, or inner lines turn muddy at small sizes, and custom colors weaken the contrast that marks the current page. (`page-controls`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Support both ways people use a page control: tapping and scrubbing.** Tapping beside the current indicator shows the next or previous page, an iPadOS pointer can target a dot, and scrubbing moves in sequence or past an edge to the first or last page. (`page-controls`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Animate page changes for taps, not while people scrub.** People can scrub very quickly, and animating every transition makes the app lag and flash distractingly. (`page-controls`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Choose the background style by the control's role.** Automatic shows the background only during interaction, for a control that isn't the primary navigation. Prominent always shows it, only when the control is the primary navigation. Minimal never does, just marking position. (`page-controls`)
- **(iOS, iPadOS) Don't support scrubbing with the minimal background style.** It gives no visual feedback while people scrub, so use the automatic or prominent style to allow scrubbing. (`page-controls`)
- **(macOS) Put a path control in the window body, never in a toolbar or status bar.** It shows the selected item's path, or the window's folder when nothing is selected, as the Finder's path bar does at the bottom of its window body. (`path-controls`)
- **(macOS) Choose between the standard and pop-up path control styles.** Standard lists the root disk, parent folders, and selected item with icons and names, hiding middle names when long. Pop-up shows the selected item and opens a menu of its path. Editable controls accept dragged items. (`path-controls`)

## Search fields (`search-fields`)

- **Build search on a search field: a text field with a Search icon, a Clear button, and placeholder text.** A scope bar and tokens can narrow the search before or after people make it. (`search-fields`)
- **Use placeholder text to say what people can search for.** It reinforces the scope or teaches people what content search can reach. (`search-fields`)
- **Search as people type, when possible.** Results refine continuously as the text gets more specific, so search feels more responsive. (`search-fields`)
- **Consider suggested search terms: recent searches before typing and predictions during it.** Suggestions help people search faster even when the search itself doesn't start immediately. (`search-fields`)
- **Simplify search results: put the most relevant first, and consider categorizing them.** People then need to scroll less to find what they're looking for. (`search-fields`)
- **Consider letting people filter results, for example with a scope bar in the results area.** Filters help people narrow the results quickly and easily. (`search-fields`)
- **Use a scope bar to move between clearly defined categories, defaulting to the broader scope.** Mail on iPhone narrows from every mailbox to the one being viewed, and a broad default gives context for the full set of results. (`search-fields`)
- **Use tokens to filter by common terms or items, and pair them with search suggestions.** A token is a selectable, editable unit, like a contact in Mail or photos in Messages, and suggestions teach people which tokens exist. (`search-fields`)
- **(iOS) Choose among three search entry points: a tab, a toolbar at the bottom or top, or a field inline with content.** The right place depends on the app's layout, content, and navigation. (`search-fields`)
- **(iOS) Make search a tab to keep it visible and available in every section, in the standard or button style.** The standard tab matches the other tabs and opens a landing page with the field at the top, while the button appearance stands apart and raises the keyboard at once. (`search-fields`)
- **(iOS) Use the standard tab style for exploration and the button appearance for quick lookups.** The landing page can reveal suggestions and categories, as the Apple TV app shows genres, while the button returns people to their previous tab when they exit search. (`search-fields`)
- **(iOS) In a toolbar, prefer search at the bottom when there's room.** It keeps search easy to reach, as the only item in Settings or beside other controls in Mail and Notes. A bottom field or button animates into a field above the keyboard. (`search-fields`)
- **(iOS) Put search at the top when content at the bottom matters more or there's no bottom toolbar.** Wallet keeps its event passes stacked at the bottom, and the top toolbar button animates into a field above the keyboard or at the top. (`search-fields`)
- **(iOS) Use an inline search field when sitting beside its content shows it searches only that content.** It suits apps with several search fields where location sets the scope, like the Music library filter beside the main search tab. (`search-fields`)
- **(iOS) Put a top inline search field above the list it searches, and consider pinning it to the top toolbar on scroll.** That keeps it distinct from search elsewhere in the app. (`search-fields`)
- **(iPadOS, macOS) Keep the search experience consistent between iPad and Mac.** Placement and behavior of the search field are similar on both platforms. (`search-fields`)
- **(iPadOS, macOS) Put a search field at the trailing end of the toolbar for many common uses.** It suits split views like Mail, Notes, and Voice Memos, where people navigate results while the selection stays visible, and apps whose results fill the detail view, like Freeform. (`search-fields`)
- **(iPadOS, macOS) Put search atop the sidebar to filter the sidebar's content or navigation.** Settings uses it to expose deeply nested sections, and it separates the filtered sidebar from a rich detail view. (`search-fields`)
- **(iPadOS, macOS) Make search its own sidebar item or tab when it needs an area for discovery.** Music and TV use one to feature suggestions, categories, and recent searches, and it keeps search available as people switch sections. (`search-fields`)
- **(iPadOS, macOS) Consider focusing the field immediately when people open a dedicated search area.** It helps them find the field and search faster, except on iPad with only a virtual keyboard, where the keyboard would unexpectedly cover the view. (`search-fields`)
- **(iPadOS, macOS) Place the search field with window resizing in mind.** On iPad it resizes fluidly as on Mac, and in compact views it must stay where it's most useful, as Notes and Mail move search above the content list column. (`search-fields`)

## Searching (`searching`)

- **If search matters, give it a primary position.** Notes puts it in the bottom toolbar beside other important actions, and tab-based apps like Photos and the Apple TV app make it a dedicated tab. (`searching`)
- **Make all content searchable from one clearly identified location.** Apps with clearly distinct sections may add a local search, like the iOS Music app's filter for songs and albums. (`searching`)
- **Always show the current scope of a search.** Use descriptive placeholder text, a scope bar, or a title, as Mail always shows which mailbox is being searched. (`searching`)
- **Provide suggestions to make searching easier, personalized from earlier searches when it makes sense.** Recent searches before typing and predictive suggestions while typing help people search faster and type less, and completions or corrections can help too. (`searching`)
- **Consider privacy before showing search history, and let people clear it.** People might not want others to see what they searched for. (`searching`)
- **Let people scope a search or filter results by attributes like creation date, file size, or file type.** In iOS, iPadOS, and macOS apps, also help people find content within an open document or window. (`searching`)
- **Make the app's content searchable in Spotlight with descriptive metadata.** Spotlight indexes it for fast searches across all apps and the web, so people find the content without opening the app. (`searching`)
- **Describe the metadata of custom file types you handle.** A Spotlight File Importer plug-in tells Spotlight what metadata your format carries. (`searching`)
- **Consider Spotlight for advanced file search within the app.** A button can start a Spotlight search based on the current selection, and a custom view can show the results or a filtered subset. (`searching`)
- **Prefer the system open and save views, and provide Quick Look previews for custom file types.** The system views include a search field for the whole system, and a Quick Look generator lets Spotlight and other apps preview your documents. (`searching`)
